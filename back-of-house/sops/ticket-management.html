Ticket Management

Role: Line Cook · Opener Category: BOH SOP Updated: May 2026
Allergen tickets come first, always. If a ticket has an allergen flag, read it before anything else on the rail. Never fire an allergen dish alongside a standard order without a clean setup.

Step 1

Read every ticket before you touch anything

When a ticket drops, read the full ticket — table number, cover count, modifiers, cooking temperatures, and any allergen flags — before picking up a single pan. Misread tickets cause remakes, delays, and allergen risks. Slow down to read once rather than rush and re-fire.

Step 2

Call your section

When you take a ticket, call it out so expo and adjacent stations know you have it. Use the ticket number or table number clearly: "Table 7, two covers, on." If you're running behind on a ticket already on the rail, say so immediately — "Table 4, holding" — so expo can manage the pace with FOH instead of being surprised at the pass.

Step 3

Fire courses in sequence — confirm timing with expo

Never fire mains without a starters-away confirmation from expo. When you're ready to fire the next course, call it: "Ready to fire mains, table 7." Expo will confirm or hold based on the pace of the room. Do not fire on your own judgement if expo has not given the go-ahead — the pass controls timing, not the station.

Step 4

Handle allergen tickets with a separate setup

Any ticket flagged with an allergen requires a clean station before you start. Wipe down and sanitise your surface, use clean equipment pulled from storage — not the shared prep area — and plate on a dish you have confirmed is clean. Call the allergen out when you bring it to the pass: "Allergen dish, table 5, cover 2 — no nuts." This call happens every time, even if expo already knows.

Step 5

If a ticket is unclear, hold and clarify — never guess

If a modifier is ambiguous, the cover count doesn't match what's on the rail, or an allergen flag looks incomplete, hold the ticket and ask expo before firing. The cost of a 30-second clarification is always lower than a remake or a guest receiving the wrong dish. Call it: "Hold on table 9 — checking a modifier."
