BOH Service · Back of House

Kitchen Communication

Department / Area Back of House · BOH Service
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Kitchen Communication defines the required standard, sequence, and controls for this procedure.

1

Purpose

This SOP defines the required standard for kitchen communication so the team follows the same safe, consistent process every shift.

2

Scope

Applies to All Kitchen and any team member assigned to this procedure, related checks, or manager follow-up.

3

Reference

Kitchens run on short, clear, confirmed calls. Learn the language, use it consistently, and respond to it — every time.

Expediting Language

These calls are used in service. Know them before your first shift on the line.

CallMeaning
FireStart cooking the next course for this table now.
HoldDo not fire — the table is not ready. Wait for the next call.
All dayTotal count of a dish across all active tickets. "Three salmon all day."
WalkingThe dish is plated and being brought to the pass now.
Pick upThe dish is at the pass — runner is needed immediately.
WindowDish is at the pass and has been there — it needs to move now.
On the flyPriority re-fire — something was wrong and this replaces it urgently.
BehindYou are moving behind someone. Call it before you move, not after.
CornerYou are about to turn a corner. Always call this when carrying hot items.
Hot behindYou are carrying something very hot. Call it loudly and clearly.

86 Protocol

Timing Between Stations

Kitchen-to-FOH Communication

4

Procedure

When you run out - Tell expo immediately — not when the next ticket arrives

The moment you know a dish is 86'd — last portion used, stock not available, ingredient pulled for food safety — tell expo out loud: "86 sea bass." Expo tells the manager. The manager updates FOH. FOH updates the floor. This chain must happen in under two minutes. If FOH finds out by trying to order the dish, the chain failed somewhere.

Calling times - Let adjacent stations know where you are

If your component takes longer than others on the same ticket, tell the other stations early — not when they're already plating. Call your time clearly: "Grill, two minutes on the rib." The other cook adjusts. If you don't call your time, the pass ends up with half a table and a cold plate waiting for you. Call early, call clearly, and respond when other stations call theirs.

At the pass - What to say — and what not to say — to runners

When calling a runner, give the table number and any critical information only: "Table 7, four covers." For allergen dishes: "Table 5, cover 2 — no nuts, confirmed clean setup." Do not explain how the dish was cooked, what went wrong earlier, or give commentary. Runners relay what you say. Keep it short, correct, and repeatable. If a runner asks a question you can't answer — "What's in this sauce?" — the answer is to get expo or the chef, not to guess.

5

Standards

Follow Sequence Complete steps in order
Stop If Unsure Ask the manager before guessing
Keep Clean Reset tools and surfaces after use
6

Corrective Action

If the standard cannot be met

Stop the task, tell the manager, isolate any affected food, equipment, or records, and follow the corrective action process before resuming.

If food safety may be affected

Do not serve or store the affected item until a manager confirms the next action. Discard when safety cannot be verified.

7

Records

Record any failed check, corrective action, manager decision, or required sign-off before the end of the shift.
