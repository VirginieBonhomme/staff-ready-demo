Welcome to the Team

New Hire Onboarding All Roles Updated: May 2026

This is your starting point. Before you look at anything role-specific, read this page. It covers what is expected of every person who works here, regardless of position. These are not suggestions — they are the baseline standard.

Once you have read through everything on this page, scroll to the bottom and choose your role. Your role guide walks you through exactly what to read, in what order, over your first week. At the end of the week, you sign off. That sign-off goes to your manager and confirms that you were given the information.

Expected of Everyone — From Day One

These standards apply before you know every detail of your role.

Some things are not role-specific. They are not things you learn after settling in. They apply on your first shift.

Handwashing Wash hands before handling food, after touching raw proteins, after using the restroom, after touching your face or phone, after handling rubbish, and when returning from any break. Minimum 20 seconds with soap. This applies to kitchen and front-of-house staff. Read the Handwashing SOP before your first shift.
Allergens The Big 9 allergens can cause life-threatening reactions. If a guest declares an allergy and you are not completely certain how a dish is prepared, you do not guess — you find out. In the kitchen, allergen-flagged tickets trigger a separate preparation process. In FOH, every allergy is entered in the POS and verbally confirmed with the kitchen. Read the Allergen Handling SOP before your first shift.
Illness & personal health Do not come to work if you have vomiting, diarrhoea, jaundice, or a fever. These are not symptoms you push through — they are legal exclusion triggers. Call your manager as early as possible if you are unwell. You must be symptom-free for 48 hours before returning to work in a food-handling role. Read the Employee Health Policy.
Uniform & presentation Clean uniform every shift, hair tied back or covered in BOH, no jewellery or nail varnish on food-handling staff. FOH has its own uniform standard — your manager will brief you. These are not optional on any shift.
Communication Call out when you are behind. Call out when something is wrong. Call out when you do not understand. Silence during service means something has been missed. No one expects you to know everything in your first week — but everyone expects you to speak up when you are unsure rather than guess.
Asking for help A new hire who does not ask questions in their first week is not impressing anyone. They are creating problems that will surface later. Ask early. Ask your trainer, your manager, or any senior team member. There are no wrong questions in week one — only wrong silences.
No phone at the station Phones do not belong at prep stations, on the line, or on the bar during service. If you need your phone, step away. Using a phone at a food prep station is a hygiene failure — raw proteins, open containers, and a phone screen are a contamination risk.
Punctuality Your shift time is the time you arrive ready to work — not the time you walk through the door. Arrive before your start time. If you are running late, call before your shift starts. Late arrivals with no notice affect every other person on the team.
Common Tasks Every New Hire Should Know

These come up in every role, every shift.

Where the handwashing sink is — and that it is always stocked with soap and paper towels
How to report an illness or symptom before your shift — who to call and how early
What an allergen flag on a ticket or order means — and that it is never ignored or guessed
Where to find the allergen information for every dish — ask your manager on day one
What to do if you injure yourself — clean, cover with a blue detectable plaster, rewash hands, tell your manager
How to raise a concern during service without disrupting the floor or the kitchen — speak to your shift lead quietly
Where fire exits are located — your manager will walk you through this on day one
Who is in charge this shift and how to contact them — know before service starts
What to do in a guest emergency — stay calm, tell the manager immediately, follow their direction
