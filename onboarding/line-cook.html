Line Cook — Your First Week

Role: Line Cook New Hire Path Updated: May 2026

This is your path through the first week. Not a general orientation — a day-by-day reading list specific to this role that tells you exactly what to read, in what order, before you touch anything. Follow it in sequence. Each SOP takes about three minutes to read and will make your shift less confusing than if you hadn't read it.

At the end of the week, you sign off to confirm you've read and understood everything. That sign-off goes to your manager. It is not a test. It is a record that you were given the information.

Before Your First Shift

Three reads. Ten minutes. Do this before you arrive.

These three SOPs are non-negotiable starting points. They cover hygiene, allergens, and knife handling — the three areas where a mistake on day one causes a serious problem. Read them before you walk in. You will be expected to know them.

Read in order 01 Handwashing Procedure ~2 min 02 Knife Safety & Storage ~3 min 03 Allergen Handling ~4 min

By the time you arrive for your first shift, you should be able to name the Big 9 allergens from memory, explain why cross-contact is different from cross-contamination, and know where knives are stored and how they are passed safely.

Day One

Observe the shift. Ask questions. Do not freelance.

Your first shift is about orientation, not output. You will shadow your trainer through service. Watch how the station is set up, how handoff happens between positions, and how communication runs on the pass. You are not expected to work independently. You are expected to be present, ask questions when you do not understand something, and not improvise.

You will be shown the physical layout of everything. Confirm each of these with your trainer before service starts:

Where knives are stored and how they go back
Where the probe thermometer is kept and how to calibrate it
Where allergen information is posted and how to flag a query
How food is labelled in the walk-in — what goes on a label and what format
Where the handwashing station is and what is always kept stocked there
How a ticket moves from the printer to the pass

At the end of day one, you should know where everything is. You do not need to be fast. You need to know where things live.

Day Two

Learn your station before service starts.

Before your second shift, read these three SOPs. They cover your station setup, the fryer, and grill cleaning. On day two you will begin to assist with setup under supervision. You will not be running a station independently — but you will be expected to understand what correct looks like so you can do it right when your trainer steps back.

Read before this shift 04 Cold Prep Setup ~4 min 05 Fryer Startup & Shutdown ~3 min 06 Grill Cleaning Protocol ~3 min

By the end of day two, you should be able to set up the cold prep station without being told what goes where, and explain the correct sequence for fryer startup and shutdown. Your trainer will check both before they sign you off on the station.

Day Three Onward

The paperwork that keeps the kitchen legal.

By day three you should be operating your station with supervision stepping back. These reads cover temperature logging, the opening checklist, and end-of-service duties — the administrative layer of the job that new hires often miss because no one explains it directly. Missing a temp log is not a small thing. Read these before day three.

Read before day three 07 Temperature Logging ~3 min 08 Opening Checklists ~2 min 09 Closing Checklists ~2 min

You are expected to complete your own temperature logs without being reminded by day four. Your trainer will initial your first three. After that, the log is yours. If you are ever unsure whether something needs to be logged, log it and flag it — do not skip and move on.

What Is Expected of You

Standards that apply from day one, not from week two.

These are not things you will be forgiven for not knowing once you have read this page.

Hygiene Wash hands every time you move between tasks, return from a break, handle raw protein, or touch your face. Not most of the time — every time. If your trainer does not see you washing your hands, that is not permission to skip it.
Allergens If a customer flags an allergy and you are not certain how a dish is made, you do not guess. You stop, you ask your supervisor, and the answer the customer receives comes from someone who knows. Your uncertainty is not a problem. An incorrect confident answer is.
Communication Call out when you are behind. Call out when something is wrong. Call out when you do not understand a ticket. The kitchen communicates constantly — silence during service usually means something has been missed.
Labelling Nothing goes into the walk-in unlabelled. Contents, date prepped, use-by date. Every container, every time. If you find unlabelled items, do not use them — bring them to the supervisor before service.
Asking for help You are expected to ask questions. A new hire who does not ask questions in their first week is not impressing anyone — they are creating problems that show up later. Ask early. Ask often.
End of First Week

Sign off to confirm you have read and understood this material.

This form asks you to confirm, by name, that you have read all nine SOPs and checklists listed above and that you understand the standards on this page. It takes two minutes. It is timestamped and goes to your manager automatically.

If there is anything on this page you are not clear on before you submit, go back and reread it, or ask your manager before you sign. Once submitted, this is your record that you were trained.

Complete First Week Sign-Off

Submissions are logged in the manager's onboarding sheet. If you cannot access the form, ask your manager to log it manually before the end of your first week.
