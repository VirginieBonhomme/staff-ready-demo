Prep Cook — Your First Week

Role: Prep Cook New Hire Path Updated: May 2026

Prep sets up everything that service depends on. If prep is done wrong — wrong portions, missing labels, cross-contaminated boards — the problem doesn't show up until the kitchen is full and there's no time to fix it. This path gives you what you need to do the job correctly from the start.

Read each SOP before the shift it belongs to. At the end of the week, complete the sign-off form. That form goes to your manager and confirms you were given the information.

Before Your First Shift

Three reads before you arrive.

Hygiene, knives, and allergens are the three areas that cause serious problems when someone gets them wrong on day one. Read these before you walk in. You will be expected to know them from the moment you start.

Read in order 01 Handwashing Procedure ~2 min 02 Knife Safety & Storage ~3 min 03 Allergen Handling ~4 min

Before you arrive, you should be able to name the Big 9 allergens, explain why separate cutting boards matter, and know how to pass a knife safely. These are not optional — they apply on day one.

Day One

Learn the walk-in and how things are labelled before you touch anything.

Your first shift is orientation. You will shadow your trainer through the prep session. Watch how items are pulled from the walk-in, how boards are assigned, and how prepped containers are labelled before they go back. Do not start any task until your trainer has shown you how it is done here — methods vary between kitchens and what worked somewhere else may not be how we do it.

By the end of day one, confirm you know all of these with your trainer:

What goes on a label — contents, prep date, use-by date, and who made it
Which cutting board colour is used for which food type
Where the colour-coded boards are stored and how they are cleaned between tasks
How stock is rotated in the walk-in — oldest to the front, every time
How to handle an unlabelled container you find in the walk-in
Where allergen information is posted and what to do if you are not sure about an ingredient

You are not expected to work at full speed today. You are expected to observe correctly and ask questions when something is unclear. Silence is not the right answer when you don't understand something.

Day Two

Station setup and storage — the two systems you run every day.

Before your second shift, read these two SOPs. On day two you will begin working through tasks with your trainer watching. You will be expected to set up the prep station correctly, apply labels accurately, and return items to the walk-in in the right order. Your trainer will check each of these before stepping back.

Read before this shift 04 Cold Prep Setup ~4 min 05 Dry Storage ~3 min

By the end of day two, your trainer should be able to step away and trust that your station is set up correctly and your labels are right. If they cannot, that conversation will happen before day three.

Day Three Onward

The checklist is your accountability for every opening shift.

From day three, you are expected to work through your prep list with less direct supervision. Read the opening checklist before this shift — it is the document that confirms the kitchen is ready for service. As a prep cook, you contribute directly to several items on it. Know what those items are and own them.

Read before day three 06 Opening Checklists ~2 min

By the end of your first week, your trainer should be able to give you a prep list and trust that everything on it gets done correctly, labelled correctly, and returned to the right place. That is the standard. Work toward it each day.

What Is Expected of You

Standards that apply from day one.

These are not things you will be forgiven for not knowing once you have read this page.

Labelling Nothing goes into the walk-in without a label. Contents, prep date, use-by date. Any unlabelled container you find goes to the supervisor — not into service. This applies even if it looks obvious what it is.
Board discipline Colour-coded boards are not a suggestion. Yellow for raw poultry, red for raw meat, blue for raw fish, green for veg and fruit, white for ready-to-eat. If the correct board is missing, you stop and find it — you do not substitute.
Allergens If you are ever unsure whether an ingredient is allergen-free, you do not use it and assume it is fine. You ask. Every time. An unlabelled container from a supplier is not confirmed allergen-free until it has been checked against the delivery note.
Rotation Oldest stock always goes to the front. New deliveries go behind. This is not a personal preference — it is how we avoid serving expired food. Check dates every time you pull from the walk-in, not just when you have time.
Handwashing Wash hands every time you move between raw proteins, return from a break, or touch anything that isn't food or a clean surface. Prep involves more task-switching than any other position — which means more handwashing than any other position.
End of First Week

Sign off to confirm you have read and understood this material.

This form asks you to confirm, by name, that you have read all six SOPs and checklists listed above and that you understand the standards on this page. It takes two minutes. It is timestamped and goes to your manager automatically.

If anything on this page is unclear before you submit, reread it or ask your manager. Once submitted, this is your record that you were trained.

Complete First Week Sign-Off

Submissions are logged in the manager's onboarding sheet. If you cannot access the form, ask your manager to log it manually before the end of your first week.
