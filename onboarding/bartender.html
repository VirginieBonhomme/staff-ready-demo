Bartender — Your First Week

Role: Bartender New Hire Path Updated: May 2026

This is your path through the first week behind the bar. It is not a general orientation — it is a day-by-day reading list specific to this role that tells you exactly what to read, in what order, before you work the bar alone. Follow it in sequence. Each SOP takes a few minutes to read and will make a real difference when service is moving fast and you cannot stop to ask.

At the end of the week, you sign off to confirm you have read and understood everything. That sign-off goes to your manager. It is not a test. It is a record that you were given the information.

Before Your First Shift

Three reads. Fifteen minutes. Do this before you arrive.

These three SOPs are the non-negotiable starting points for the bartender role. They cover hygiene, allergen communication, and the table service sequence. Allergens behind the bar are a real risk — nuts in cocktails, dairy in coffee, gluten in beer. Read these before you walk in. You will be expected to know them.

Read in order 01 Handwashing Procedure ~2 min 02 Allergen Communication ~4 min 03 Table Service Sequence ~4 min

By the time you arrive for your first shift, you should be able to name the Big 9 allergens, explain what steps to take when an allergy ticket comes in for a bar order, and understand the basic service sequence for drink orders made at the bar.

Day One

Shadow the bar. Learn the layout. Do not work a section alone.

Your first shift is about orientation, not speed. You will shadow your trainer through the full service. Watch how the bar is set up before doors open, how tabs and table orders are managed in the POS, and how cash and card payments are handled. You are not expected to work independently. You are expected to be present, ask questions, and not improvise behind the bar.

Confirm each of these with your trainer before service starts:

Where everything lives behind the bar — spirits, modifiers, glassware, tools, and ice wells
How to log into the bar POS, open a tab, and route a table order
How an allergy flag is handled when a drink order comes in
Where allergen information for cocktails, coffee, and beer is kept
How cash is counted and reconciled — never alone, always with a witness
What to do if a tap blows or a piece of equipment fails mid-service
Who is shift lead today and when to escalate

At the end of day one, you should know where everything lives, how the POS handles tabs and table orders, and what the correct allergen process looks like for a bar order. You do not need to be fast. You need to know the process.

Day Two

Learn the money and the difficult moments.

Before your second shift, read these two SOPs. They cover payment and cash-up, and complaints and recovery. The bar handles more payment transactions than any other role on the floor — and cash errors are the ones that matter at end of shift. On day two you will begin to assist with orders under supervision. You are not working the bar independently yet, but you will be expected to understand what correct looks like before you are in it.

Read before this shift 04 Payment & Cash-Up ~3 min 05 Complaints & Recovery ~4 min 06 Bartender Checklist ~3 min

By the end of day two, you should know how cash is counted at opening and reconciled at close, what to do when a guest raises a complaint about a drink, and what your bar opening and closing duties are. Your trainer will walk you through your first cash reconciliation.

Day Three Onward

The routines that run before and after every service.

By day three you should be assisting with bar orders and preparing to work sections with supervision stepping back. These reads cover the full FOH opening checklist and the FOH closing checklist — the structural routines every bartender is responsible for contributing to. A bar that is not set up correctly before service creates problems for everyone. A bar that is not closed down correctly creates problems for the next shift.

Read before day three 07 FOH Opening Checklist ~3 min 08 FOH Closing Checklist ~3 min 09 Pre-Service Brief ~2 min

You are expected to have the bar fully set up — ice, garnish, glassware, POS, and cash drawer confirmed — before the pre-service brief starts. By day four you should be completing the bar opening setup independently without being prompted.

What Is Expected of You

Standards that apply from day one, not from week two.

These are not things you will be forgiven for not knowing once you have read this page.

Allergen accuracy When an allergy flag comes in on a bar order, you stop and check the drink against the allergen information before making it. You use a clean glass and clean tools — no shared shakers, no reused jiggers. You never assume a cocktail modifier or mixer is allergen-free. If you are not certain, you confirm with the manager before the drink goes out.
Cash counted twice The cash drawer is counted at opening with a manager witness and reconciled at close with a manager witness. You never count alone. Any variance — over or short — is logged immediately and handed to the manager before you leave. A variance discovered after the fact is harder to explain and harder to fix.
Bar top standards The bar top is wiped after every transaction. No empty glasses in front of guests. No spills left to sit. A clean bar tells guests they are in good hands. A sticky bar tells them the opposite. This applies throughout service — not just during quiet moments.
Ice never runs low Ice wells are filled to full before service and restocked throughout the shift. The bar cannot function without ice. If an ice well is below half during service, you refill it now — not when you get a moment. Running out of ice during a rush is an avoidable problem.
Asking for help Ask your trainer or the shift lead before you guess — especially with allergens, payment discrepancies, or equipment failures. The wrong confident decision behind the bar has consequences for guests and for the reconciliation at close. There are no wrong questions in week one.
End of First Week

Sign off to confirm you have read and understood this material.

This form asks you to confirm, by name, that you have read all nine SOPs and checklists listed above and that you understand the standards on this page. It takes two minutes. It is timestamped and goes to your manager automatically.

If there is anything on this page you are not clear on before you submit, go back and reread it, or ask your manager before you sign. Once submitted, this is your record that you were trained.

Complete First Week Sign-Off

Submissions are logged in the manager's onboarding sheet. If you cannot access the form, ask your manager to log it manually before the end of your first week.
