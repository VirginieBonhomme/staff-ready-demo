Server — Your First Week

Role: Server New Hire Path Updated: May 2026

This is your path through the first week as a server. It is not a general orientation — it is a day-by-day reading list specific to this role that tells you exactly what to read, in what order, before you take a table alone. Follow it in sequence. Each SOP takes a few minutes to read and will make your first shifts significantly less stressful.

At the end of the week, you sign off to confirm you have read and understood everything. That sign-off goes to your manager. It is not a test. It is a record that you were given the information.

Before Your First Shift

Three reads. Fifteen minutes. Do this before you arrive.

These three SOPs are the non-negotiable starting points for this role. They cover hygiene, allergen communication, and the table service sequence — the three areas where a mistake in the first week causes a real problem for a guest. Read them before you walk in. You will be expected to know them from the start.

Read in order 01 Handwashing Procedure ~2 min 02 Allergen Communication ~4 min 03 Table Service Sequence ~4 min

By the time you arrive for your first shift, you should be able to explain how an allergy is entered in the POS and confirmed with the kitchen, and know the correct sequence for greeting, taking orders, and closing a table.

Day One

Shadow the section. Observe everything. Do not take a table alone.

Your first shift is about orientation, not output. You will shadow your trainer through the full service. Watch how the section is set up before doors open, how orders move from table to POS to kitchen, and how the team communicates during a rush. You are not expected to work independently. You are expected to be attentive, ask questions when you do not follow something, and not improvise.

Confirm each of these with your trainer before service starts:

Where your section is and how it is laid out on the floor plan
How to log into the POS and how to select a table
Where allergen information for every dish is kept and how to flag a guest allergy
How a guest allergy is entered in the POS and communicated to the kitchen
Where the water station, linen, and cutlery are stocked for your section
How to fire a course and how to call away
Who is shift lead today and how to reach them during service

At the end of day one, you should know where everything is, how the POS works at a basic level, and how allergen flags are handled. You do not need to be fast. You need to know the process.

Day Two

Learn how to handle the difficult moments.

Before your second shift, read these two SOPs. They cover complaints and recovery, and payment and cash-up — the two areas new servers most often get wrong because they are uncomfortable situations, not technical ones. On day two you will begin to assist with tables under supervision. You will not be running a full section independently yet, but you will be expected to understand what the correct response is before you are in it.

Read before this shift 04 Complaints & Recovery ~4 min 05 Payment & Cash-Up ~3 min 06 Server Checklist ~3 min

By the end of day two, you should know what to say when a guest complains about a dish, how to process a bill correctly, and what your opening and closing duties are for your section. Your trainer will walk you through your first bill close.

Day Three Onward

The operational layer that ties the shift together.

By day three you should be assisting with tables and preparing to take your own section. These reads cover the FOH opening checklist, the closing checklist, and the pre-service brief — the structural parts of every shift that keep the floor running before and after guests arrive. Understanding your role in these routines makes you useful before and after service, not just during it.

Read before day three 07 FOH Opening Checklist ~3 min 08 FOH Closing Checklist ~3 min 09 Pre-Service Brief ~2 min

You are expected to attend every pre-service brief from day one and come with your section ready. By day four you should be completing your own section setup independently without being prompted by your trainer.

What Is Expected of You

Standards that apply from day one, not from week two.

These are not things you will be forgiven for not knowing once you have read this page.

Allergen entry Every guest allergy is entered in the POS on that order. Every time. Not noted on a pad and passed verbally — entered in the system and confirmed with the kitchen before the order is placed. If you are not sure how the dish is prepared, you do not guess. You find out before the guest orders.
Complaint handling When a guest raises a complaint, you listen, you apologise without deflecting, and you tell the manager immediately. You do not offer free items without manager approval. You do not argue or justify the kitchen. You escalate, follow up, and close the loop so the guest knows it was addressed.
Bill accuracy Every bill is checked before it goes to the table. Overcharges and undercharges both need to be corrected before the guest sees them — not after. If there is a discrepancy you cannot explain, ask your manager before presenting the bill.
Section readiness Your section is your responsibility. Water station stocked, cutlery set, menus clean, POS working — all of this before the first guest arrives. A guest sitting at a table that is not set is never acceptable. Check before service, not after the first table is seated.
Asking for help Ask your trainer, your shift lead, or a senior server before you guess. The wrong answer to a guest question is worse than saying "let me check on that for you." Every experienced server was new once — no question is a stupid question in week one.
End of First Week

Sign off to confirm you have read and understood this material.

This form asks you to confirm, by name, that you have read all nine SOPs and checklists listed above and that you understand the standards on this page. It takes two minutes. It is timestamped and goes to your manager automatically.

If there is anything on this page you are not clear on before you submit, go back and reread it, or ask your manager before you sign. Once submitted, this is your record that you were trained.

Complete First Week Sign-Off

Submissions are logged in the manager's onboarding sheet. If you cannot access the form, ask your manager to log it manually before the end of your first week.
