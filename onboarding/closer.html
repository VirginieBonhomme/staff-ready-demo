Closer — Your First Week

Role: Closer New Hire Path Updated: May 2026

The closer is the last person responsible for the kitchen. When the close is done correctly, the opener walks in the next morning and everything is exactly where it needs to be. When it is not — a grill left dirty, knives not stored, a fridge door not shut — that problem lands on everyone the next day.

This path tells you what to read and in what order before each shift of your first week. At the end, sign off to confirm you have the information. That record goes to your manager.

Before Your First Shift

Three reads before you arrive.

Closing involves the three highest-risk end-of-day tasks: cleaning hot equipment, storing knives correctly, and logging final temperatures. Read these three SOPs before you walk in. Each one covers something that goes wrong when it is done from memory rather than from understanding the why.

Read in order 01 Handwashing Procedure ~2 min 02 Knife Safety & Storage ~3 min 03 Grill Cleaning Protocol ~4 min

Before you arrive, you should know how to pass a knife safely and where knives are stored overnight. You should also know the correct sequence for grill cleaning — what order the steps go in and what the safety considerations are when working around hot surfaces at the end of service.

Day One

Shadow the full closing sequence in order — timing and sequence both matter.

Your first shift is observation. You follow the current closer through every task, in the sequence they do it. Closing has to happen in a specific order — certain tasks cannot start until others are finished, and some things need time (a grill needs to cool to a safe cleaning temperature, for example). Watch why the sequence is what it is, not just what it is.

Confirm all of the following with your trainer before you leave:

When grill cleaning starts relative to end of service — the cooling window before it is safe to clean
What cleaning products are used on the grill and where they are stored
Where all knives go at the end of service — the magnetic strip, sheathed, with nothing left on boards or surfaces
Which fridges are checked and logged at close and what the target temperatures are
How and where leftover prepped items are labelled before going back into the walk-in
Where the closing checklist form is submitted and who receives it

By the end of day one, you should know the full closing sequence in order. You are not expected to do it independently — but you should be able to describe it.

Day Two

Temperature logging and storage — the two administrative tasks at close.

Closing temperature logs are the end-of-day record that the kitchen stayed within safe limits during service. Dry storage needs to be checked and left in order before you walk out. Read both SOPs before your second shift. On day two, you will complete these tasks alongside your trainer.

Read before this shift 04 Temperature Logging ~3 min 05 Dry Storage ~3 min

By the end of day two, you should be completing your closing temperature log without prompting and leaving dry storage in the correct order. Your trainer should not be reminding you to do either of these.

Day Three Onward

The closing checklist is your accountability for every shift.

From day three, you begin closing with less direct supervision. Read the closing checklist before this shift — understand every item on it, not just what it asks you to do but why it is there. When you submit that form at the end of the night, you are confirming each of those items was done correctly. That is a signed record.

Read before day three 06 Closing Checklists ~2 min

By the end of your first week, you should be completing the full closing sequence independently. Your trainer should be available but not running the sequence for you. The checklist form goes in before you leave — not the next morning.

What Is Expected of You

Standards that apply from day one.

Completeness The closing checklist is not a reference for what to do when you have time. It is the minimum. Every item on it gets done every shift. If something cannot be completed — equipment failure, a missing product — you flag it to the manager before you leave, not via message the next morning.
Grill safety Do not start cleaning the grill until it has cooled to the safe cleaning temperature. Working on a hot grill causes burns. The cleaning sequence must be followed in order. Do not improvise the chemicals or the method — both are specified in the SOP for a reason.
Knife storage Every knife goes to the magnetic strip at the end of service. No exceptions. A knife left on a board or a surface overnight is a safety hazard for the opener. Check every prep area before you leave.
Temperature logs Log the actual reading. Never estimate. If a fridge is showing a temperature outside the safe range at close, record it accurately and tell the manager immediately — they need to decide whether items need to be discarded before the kitchen opens again.
The last check Before you leave, walk the kitchen. Check that everything is off, everything is stored, and nothing has been left out. This is not a formality. It is the reason the closer exists as a role.
End of First Week

Sign off to confirm you have read and understood this material.

This form asks you to confirm, by name, that you have read all six SOPs and checklists listed above and understand the standards on this page. It is timestamped and goes directly to your manager.

If anything on this page is unclear before you submit, reread it or ask your manager first. Once submitted, this is the record that you were trained.

Complete First Week Sign-Off

Submissions are logged in the manager's onboarding sheet. If you cannot access the form, ask your manager to log it manually before the end of your first week.
