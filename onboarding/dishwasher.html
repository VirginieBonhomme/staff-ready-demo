Dishwasher — Your First Week

Role: Dishwasher New Hire Path Updated: May 2026

The dish area controls whether the kitchen has clean plates, pans, and tools when service needs them. When the dish area is running well, nobody thinks about it. When it falls behind — or when clean and dirty items are mixed up — every position in the kitchen feels it immediately.

This path gives you the reads and the orientation to run the dish area correctly from day one. Follow it in sequence. Sign off at the end of the week to confirm you have the information.

Before Your First Shift

Two reads before you arrive.

The dish area operates a commercial machine that runs at high temperature and pressure. It is not complicated, but it has a specific correct sequence — deviating from it causes equipment damage or means items come out contaminated and looking clean. Read these before you walk in.

Read in order 01 Handwashing Procedure ~2 min 02 Dish Machine Operation ~4 min

Before you arrive, you should know the startup sequence for the dish machine, what the correct wash and rinse temperatures are, and why items must air-dry rather than be towel-dried. You should also know when to wash hands in a role where you are constantly handling dirty items then clean ones.

Day One

Learn the flow — dirty side, clean side, and how items move between them.

The fundamental rule of the dish area is separation. Dirty and clean items never occupy the same space or touch each other. Your first shift is about understanding that flow — how dirty items come in, how they move through the machine, and how clean items go back out to the kitchen. Your trainer will show you the physical layout. Watch the movement, not just the tasks.

Confirm all of the following with your trainer before you leave after day one:

Which side of the dish area is the dirty side and which is the clean side — and why they never cross
How to load the machine correctly — which way items face, what does and does not go in the machine
How to check that the machine has reached the correct wash and rinse temperatures before starting
How long items need to air-dry before they go back to the kitchen — and why towel-drying is not acceptable
Where clean items go when they come out — specific locations for plates, pans, utensils
What to do when the machine is backed up during service — how to communicate that to the kitchen

By the end of day one, you should understand the flow of the dish area and be able to operate the machine correctly with your trainer watching. Speed comes with time. The sequence comes from day one.

Day Two

Three-sink sanitizing — for what the machine cannot handle.

Not everything goes through the dish machine. Certain items — large pans, equipment parts, containers that need sanitizing rather than just washing — go through the three-sink process. Read this SOP before your second shift. The chemicals and sequence are specific, and getting them wrong means items that look clean are not sanitized.

Read before this shift 03 Three-Sink Sanitizing ~4 min

By the end of day two, you should know which items go through the three-sink process rather than the machine, and what the correct concentration is for the sanitizing solution. Your trainer will ask you both before you start day two.

Day Three Onward

Closing the dish area and resetting dry storage.

From day three, you begin working with less direct supervision. Read these two SOPs before your third shift. The dish area close-down is as specific as the operation — the machine needs to be drained, cleaned, and left correctly so it is ready the next morning. Dry storage is your responsibility to keep in order throughout the shift.

Read before day three 04 Dish Area Closing ~3 min 05 Dry Storage ~3 min

By the end of your first week, you should be running the dish area independently through service and completing the close-down without being directed through each step. The dish area should be clean, reset, and the machine correctly shut down before you clock out.

What Is Expected of You

Standards that apply from day one.

Separation Dirty and clean items are never in the same space at the same time. This is not about tidiness — it is about contamination. A clean plate that has touched a dirty surface is no longer clean. Once you understand this, the physical layout of the dish area makes sense.
Handwashing You handle dirty items constantly. You also handle clean items constantly. Handwashing between these is not optional. The dish area is the highest-traffic handwashing position in the kitchen. Wash every time you move from dirty to clean — not most times, every time.
Machine checks Before you run the first rack of the day, check that the machine has reached the correct wash and rinse temperatures. A machine that looks like it is running but has not reached temp is washing items in warm water — not sanitizing them. Check it. Log it if required.
Communication When the dish area gets backed up, the kitchen needs to know. Do not silently fall behind and hope to catch up. Tell your nearest supervisor when you are behind so that service can adjust. Silence during a backup makes the problem worse, not better.
Knives Knives are never loaded into the dish machine. They are washed by hand, sheathed, and returned to the magnetic strip. A knife in a rack is invisible to whoever is loading the machine and will cause a serious injury. If you receive a knife in the dish area, wash it by hand immediately and return it.
