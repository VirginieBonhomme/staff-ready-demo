Opener — Your First Week

Role: Opener New Hire Path Updated: May 2026

The opener arrives before anyone else and leaves the kitchen ready for service. When that work is done correctly, nobody notices. When it is not — a missed temperature log, a fryer not at temp, stock that hasn't been rotated — everyone downstream pays for it during service.

This path gives you the reads and the orientation you need to open independently by the end of your first week. Follow it in order. Sign off at the end. That form confirms to your manager that you have the information.

Before Your First Shift

Three reads before you arrive.

The opener is the first person responsible for food safety compliance each day. Temperature logging, handwashing, and storage rotation happen before anyone else gets in. Read these three SOPs before your first shift so you understand what you are accountable for when you walk through the door.

Read in order 01 Handwashing Procedure ~2 min 02 Temperature Logging ~3 min 03 Dry Storage ~3 min

Before you arrive, you should understand what a temperature log records, how often it is completed, and what the legal minimum requirements are. You should also know what FIFO means and why it is the opener's responsibility to check rotation before service starts.

Day One

Shadow the full opening sequence in order — do not skip steps.

Your first shift is observation. You follow the current opener through every task, in the exact sequence they do it, and you take note of the order. The opening sequence has a logic to it — temperatures are checked before equipment is started, equipment is started before food is pulled, food is pulled before the station is set. Understand why the order matters, not just what the order is.

Confirm all of the following with your trainer before you leave after day one:

Where the temperature log lives and how to fill it in correctly
Which fridges and freezers are logged, and in what order
What to do if a fridge reading is outside the safe range when you arrive
Where the probe thermometer is stored, how it is calibrated, and who to tell if it is missing
How dry storage is checked — what correct rotation looks like and what to flag
Where the opening checklist form is submitted and who receives it

By the end of day one, you should be able to describe the full opening sequence from memory and know where every piece of equipment you will operate is located.

Day Two

Equipment startups — fryer and station setup.

The fryer and cold prep station are the two systems you start and confirm are ready before service. Read both SOPs before your second shift. On day two, you will perform these startups with your trainer present. You will not do anything unsupervised yet — but you will be expected to know the correct sequence before you touch the equipment.

Read before this shift 04 Fryer Startup & Shutdown ~3 min 05 Cold Prep Setup ~4 min

By the end of day two, your trainer should be confident that you can light the fryer correctly, confirm it reaches service temperature, and set up the cold prep station in the right order without being told what goes where.

Day Three Onward

The opening checklist is your accountability document.

From day three, you begin running the opening sequence with your trainer stepping back. Read the opening checklist before this shift — not as a reminder of what you already know, but to understand every item on it and why it is there. When you submit that form each morning, you are confirming that each of those items was done correctly. That is a legal record.

Read before day three 06 Opening Checklists ~2 min

By the end of your first week, you should be opening independently with your trainer available but not directing each task. The checklist form should be submitted before service starts every day. If something prevents you completing a task, you flag it to your manager — you do not skip it and submit anyway.

What Is Expected of You

Standards that apply from day one.

Temperature logs Every fridge and freezer is logged at opening, every day, before anything else. The log is a legal document. Missing a log is not a minor oversight — it is a gap in the kitchen's food safety record. If a unit is out of range, you record the actual reading, not an estimated one, and you tell the manager immediately.
Fryer safety The fryer startup sequence exists because shortcuts around hot oil cause serious burns. Follow it in order every time. If anything about the fryer looks or sounds wrong during startup, turn it off and get your manager — do not continue.
Rotation Checking and correcting stock rotation at opening is your responsibility. Do not assume the closer did it. Check it. Move older stock to the front yourself. If you find items past their use-by date, remove them and tell your manager before service.
Time The opening sequence has to be complete before service starts. That means arriving on time and working through the checklist in order without interruptions. If you are running behind on a task, communicate it — do not hope to catch up silently.
The checklist form Submit the form before service, not after. If you cannot access the Google Form, write down what you completed and tell your manager. A completed checklist that was not submitted is still a gap in the record.
End of First Week

Sign off to confirm you have read and understood this material.

This form asks you to confirm, by name, that you have read all six SOPs and checklists listed above and understand the standards on this page. It is timestamped and goes directly to your manager.

If anything on this page is unclear before you submit, reread it or ask your manager first. Once submitted, this is the record that you were trained.

Complete First Week Sign-Off

Submissions are logged in the manager's onboarding sheet. If you cannot access the form, ask your manager to log it manually before the end of your first week.
