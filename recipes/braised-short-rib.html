Braised Beef Short Rib

Role: Line Cook Updated: May 2026 Category: Mains
Yield 8 portions
Portion 1 rib · 280 g
Plating time ~3 min
Braise time 3.5 hrs

Minimum safe reheat temperature: 165°F throughout. Probe the thickest part of every rib before service. This dish is braised in advance and reheated to order — the probe check in Step 5 is mandatory, not optional.

Contains Celery Gluten Milk Sulphites
Ingredients
For the braise
8 Bone-in beef short ribs Bembridges Butchery — English, bone-in, approx. 300 g each before trim
500 ml Red wine Côtes du Rhône or similar — no cooking wine
1 L Beef stock House-made dark stock — prep fridge, labelled
2 sticks Celery, rough-chopped
2 Carrots, rough-chopped
1 Brown onion, halved
4 cloves Garlic, crushed
2 tbsp Tomato paste
2 sprigs Thyme
1 Bay leaf
to season Flaky sea salt and cracked black pepper
For the creamed potato
1.2 kg Maris Piper potatoes, peeled and quartered
120 g Unsalted butter, cold and cubed Lurpak unsalted — or Charentes-Poitou if in stock
180 ml Double cream, warmed
to season Fine salt and white pepper
To plate
40 ml per plate Braising jus, reduced and strained (from the braise)
3–4 sprigs Watercress, washed and dried Vitacress — check garnish fridge
pinch Flaky sea salt to finish — Maldon

Step 1

Season and sear the ribs

Pat each rib completely dry with paper towel — surface moisture is the enemy of a good crust. Season generously on all sides with flaky salt and cracked black pepper. Heat a heavy-based pan over high heat until smoking. Sear each rib 2–3 minutes per side until deeply browned on all surfaces including the ends. Work in batches — crowding steams rather than sears. Transfer to a deep roasting tray.

The colour you build here becomes the flavour of the finished sauce. Pale ribs going into the braise means a flat jus at the end.

Step 2

Build the braise

In the same pan over medium heat, sweat the onion, carrot, celery, and garlic until softened — about 8 minutes. Add tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes until it darkens slightly. Deglaze with the red wine, scraping up all the fond from the base. Reduce by half. Pour over the ribs in the tray. Add stock until the ribs are three-quarters submerged. Tuck in the thyme and bay leaf.

Step 3

Braise covered at 300°F for 3.5 hours

Cover the tray tightly with a double layer of foil. Place in the oven at 300°F fan. Do not open for the first 3 hours. After 3 hours, check — the meat should yield when pressed with a spoon and the bone should move freely at the thin end. If not, return for 30 more minutes. When done, rest in the liquid for 20 minutes before handling.

Ribs can be braised the day before service and held overnight submerged in their liquid in the fridge. Reheat covered at 325°F for 25 minutes before service.

Step 4

Reduce the jus and make the creamed potato

Carefully lift the ribs from the liquid and set aside. Strain the braising liquid through a fine sieve into a wide saucepan, pressing the vegetables to extract everything. Reduce over medium-high heat until the jus coats the back of a spoon — roughly 30–40 minutes. Skim fat from the surface as it rises. While the jus reduces: boil the potatoes in salted water until completely tender, drain, and pass through a fine drum sieve. Beat in cold butter piece by piece until glossy, then fold through warm cream. Season with fine salt and white pepper.

Step 5

Reheat the rib for service

Place the rib in a small oven-safe dish. Spoon 60 ml of reduced jus over the top. Cover with foil and heat at 355°F for 10–12 minutes until piping hot throughout. Probe the thickest part — minimum 165°F before it leaves the kitchen. Do not reuse jus that has been sitting on the pass for more than 30 minutes.

Plating

Bowl, potato base, rib on top, jus around

Use a wide, shallow white bowl. Spoon 80 g of creamed potato just off-centre. Place the rib bone-side down, leaning slightly against the potato. Pour 40 ml of hot jus around the base — not over the rib. Finish with 3–4 sprigs of watercress against the high side of the rib and a pinch of Maldon on the meat. Wipe the rim clean before it goes to the pass.

The rib should show its seared crust on top. If it looks pale and grey from the reheat, it was not properly seared at Step 1 — do not plate it. Flag it before it leaves the kitchen.

Storage & Shelf Life
Braised ribs Store submerged in braising liquid in a labelled container in the walk-in. Use within 3 days of braise date. Discard if liquid smells fermented or rib surface is tacky.
Jus Transfer to a labelled squeeze bottle and hold at ≥ 145°F during service. Cool and refrigerate at end of service. Use within 2 days. Bring to a full boil before each service.
Creamed potato Make fresh daily. Do not store overnight — quality drop is significant. Discard any remaining at end of service.
