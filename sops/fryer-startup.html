Fryer Startup

Department / Area Back of House · Equipment
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Fryer Startup defines the required standard, sequence, and controls for this procedure.

Key Actions

  • Check oil level before switching on.
  • Set temperature dial to service setting.
  • Confirm temperature with probe thermometer.
  • Assess oil quality — color and smell.
1

Purpose

This SOP defines the required standard for fryer startup so the team follows the same safe, consistent process every shift.

2

Scope

Applies to Line Cook / Opener and any team member assigned to this procedure, related checks, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Chef, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Approved tools, equipment, utensils, and supplies required for the task.
  • Required personal protective equipment, cleaning supplies, labels, logs, or checklists.
  • Working hand sink, sanitizer, thermometer, or other food safety control equipment when applicable.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

Step 1 - Check oil level before switching on

Look at the oil level in the fryer basket. It must sit between the MIN and MAX markers on the inside of the tank — never below minimum. Running a fryer with insufficient oil will damage the heating element and creates a fire risk. If oil is low, top up with fresh oil before proceeding.

Note — Use the same oil type as what's in the tank. Do not mix oil types without checking with a supervisor.

Step 2 - Set temperature dial to service setting

Turn the temperature dial to 350°F — our standard service temperature for all fried items unless a specific item card says otherwise. The pilot light will activate. Do not use the fryer until the temperature indicator shows the oil has reached operating temperature.

Note — Allow 15–20 minutes for the fryer to reach full temperature from cold. Set it at the start of opening prep, not when service is about to start.

Step 3 - Confirm temperature with probe thermometer

Once the indicator light signals the fryer is ready, confirm oil temperature with a calibrated probe thermometer. Dip only the tip — 3 to 4 centimetres — into the oil for 10 seconds. Reading must show 340–355°F before any food goes in. Log the temperature on the opening checklist.

Note — If temp reads below 320°F after 25 minutes, the element may need servicing. Flag to manager before service.

Step 4 - Assess oil quality — color and smell

Fresh oil is pale yellow and has a clean, neutral smell. Oil that has gone past its useful life will be dark brown, visibly foamy at the surface, or smell burnt and acrid. If the oil fails the look-and-smell test, flag it for a filter or full change before service begins — don't compromise food quality or safety.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Corrective Action

If the standard cannot be met

Stop the task, tell the manager, isolate any affected food, equipment, or records, and follow the corrective action process before resuming.

If food safety may be affected

Do not serve or store the affected item until a manager confirms the next action. Discard when safety cannot be verified.

8

Quality Control / Verification

Stop If Unsure Ask the manager before guessing
Keep Clean Reset tools and surfaces after use
9

Documentation & Records

Record any failed check, corrective action, manager decision, or required sign-off before the end of the shift.
