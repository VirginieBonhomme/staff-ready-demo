Fryer Startup

Role: Line Cook / Opener Updated: May 2026 Category: Equipment

Step 1

Check oil level before switching on

Look at the oil level in the fryer basket. It must sit between the MIN and MAX markers on the inside of the tank — never below minimum. Running a fryer with insufficient oil will damage the heating element and creates a fire risk. If oil is low, top up with fresh oil before proceeding.

Note — Use the same oil type as what's in the tank. Do not mix oil types without checking with a supervisor.

Step 2

Set temperature dial to service setting

Turn the temperature dial to 350°F — our standard service temperature for all fried items unless a specific item card says otherwise. The pilot light will activate. Do not use the fryer until the temperature indicator shows the oil has reached operating temperature.

Note — Allow 15–20 minutes for the fryer to reach full temperature from cold. Set it at the start of opening prep, not when service is about to start.

Step 3

Confirm temperature with probe thermometer

Once the indicator light signals the fryer is ready, confirm oil temperature with a calibrated probe thermometer. Dip only the tip — 3 to 4 centimetres — into the oil for 10 seconds. Reading must show 340–355°F before any food goes in. Log the temperature on the opening checklist.

Note — If temp reads below 320°F after 25 minutes, the element may need servicing. Flag to manager before service.

Step 4

Assess oil quality — colour and smell

Fresh oil is pale yellow and has a clean, neutral smell. Oil that has gone past its useful life will be dark brown, visibly foamy at the surface, or smell burnt and acrid. If the oil fails the look-and-smell test, flag it for a filter or full change before service begins — don't compromise food quality or safety.
