Thermometer Calibration

Department / Area Back of House · Food Safety
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

An inaccurate thermometer gives false confidence. If your probe reads 4°F off, every food safety decision made with it is wrong. Calibrate every probe thermometer daily before first use, and any time a thermometer is dropped or suspect.

Key Actions

  • Prepare the ice-point slurry.
  • Insert the probe and wait for a stable reading.
  • Adjust or remove from service if out of tolerance.
  • Record the check before the end of the shift.
1

Purpose

This SOP defines the required standard for thermometer calibration so the team follows the same safe, consistent process every shift.

2

Scope

Applies to Opener / Manager and any team member assigned to this procedure, related checks, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Chef, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.

6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

Reference

When to Calibrate

Daily — before first temperature check of the day
After any thermometer is dropped or physically impacted
If a reading seems unusually high or low during service
After storing a thermometer for more than 48 hours unused
Whenever a new thermometer is put into service for the first time

Step 1 - Prepare the ice-point slurry

Fill a clean container or tall glass with crushed ice. Add just enough cold water to fill the gaps between the ice — the mixture should be mostly ice with just enough water to create a slurry. Let it sit for 30 seconds to stabilise. The temperature of a correct ice-point slurry is 32°F.

Note — Do not use a single ice cube in a glass of water. The water will not be 32°F. You need a dense ice slurry for an accurate reading.

Step 2 - Insert the probe and wait for a stable reading

Insert the probe into the centre of the ice slurry, making sure the tip is surrounded by ice on all sides and not touching the sides or bottom of the container. Wait until the reading stabilises — usually 20 to 30 seconds. A correctly calibrated probe should read 32°F (±2°F). If the reading is within this range, the thermometer is accurate for use.

Step 3 - Adjust or remove from service if out of tolerance

If the probe reads outside of 32°F ±2°F (i.e., 28°F or 36°F or worse), the thermometer needs adjustment. Many digital probe thermometers have a calibration button or offset setting — refer to the manufacturer instructions for your specific model. If the thermometer cannot be adjusted to read accurately, remove it from service and label it "DO NOT USE — CALIBRATION FAIL" until it is replaced or repaired.

Note — Never use a thermometer that failed calibration for food safety decisions. An incorrect probe reading is worse than no reading — it gives false assurance that food is safe when it may not be.

Step 4 - Log the calibration result

Record the calibration result in the daily temperature log: thermometer ID or description, date, reading obtained at ice point, pass or fail, and your initials. This record is required for due diligence. Inspectors may ask to see calibration records to verify that your temperature readings are taken with accurate equipment.

Note — Calibrate all probe thermometers in use, not just one. If the kitchen has three probes, all three get checked before service.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Corrective Action

If the standard cannot be met

Stop the task, tell the manager, isolate any affected food, equipment, or records, and follow the corrective action process before resuming.

If food safety may be affected

Do not serve or store the affected item until a manager confirms the next action. Discard when safety cannot be verified.

8

Quality Control / Verification

Stop If Unsure Ask the manager before guessing
Keep Clean Reset tools and surfaces after use
9

Documentation & Records

Record any failed check, corrective action, manager decision, or required sign-off before the end of the shift.
