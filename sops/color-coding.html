Colour Coding System

Role: All Kitchen Staff Updated: May 2026 Category: Hygiene

Colour coding prevents cross-contamination at the source. Every cutting board and handled utensil in this kitchen is colour-coded. Using the wrong colour transfers harmful bacteria from raw protein onto ready-to-eat food, even after washing. Know your colours — use them every time.

Colour Reference Chart
Colour
Use For
■ Red
Raw red meat (beef, lamb, pork, venison)
■ Yellow
Raw poultry (chicken, duck, turkey)
■ Blue
Raw fish and seafood
■ Green
Fresh produce, fruit, salad, and vegetables
■ White
Dairy, bread, pastry, and cooked ready-to-eat foods
■ Purple / Brown
Allergen-dedicated prep (no cross-contact)

Rule 1

One colour per task — never swap mid-prep

If you start a task on a red board, complete that task on the red board before switching. Do not move an item from a red board to a white board — the contamination moves with it. If you need to switch food categories mid-prep, swap boards completely and wash your hands and tools before continuing.

Note — This applies to utensils too. The tongs used to handle raw chicken must not be used to plate cooked chicken. Use a fresh set of tongs or wash and sanitise between uses.

Rule 2

Fridge storage follows the same colour hierarchy

The colour coding system mirrors the fridge storage order. Raw proteins are always stored below ready-to-eat foods — exactly as the colours suggest. In the fridge: raw red meat on the lowest shelf, raw poultry above it, raw fish above that, and ready-to-eat foods (white/green) on the top shelves. If your fridge has dedicated zones, keep proteins in their labelled zone only.

Shelf position
What goes here
Top shelf
Ready-to-eat, dairy, cooked food (White)
Middle shelf
Raw fish and seafood (Blue)
Lower middle shelf
Raw poultry (Yellow)
Bottom shelf
Raw red meat only (Red)

Note — All raw proteins must be stored covered and in sealed containers. Never store unwrapped raw protein directly on a shelf where drip can reach food below — even if the shelf order is correct.

Rule 3

Wash and sanitise boards between uses — not just between colours

Before switching a board from one food to another within the same colour category (e.g., two different raw beef items), wash it with hot soapy water, rinse, and sanitise with the correct sanitizer solution. A board that looks clean is not necessarily sanitised. The colour-coding system reduces risk — it does not eliminate the need to clean between uses.

Note — Report damaged boards to your manager immediately. Boards with deep score marks, warping, or discolouration harbour bacteria in the grooves and cannot be effectively sanitised. They must be replaced.

Rule 4

Store boards in designated positions only

At the end of each shift, boards are washed, sanitised, and stored vertically in the designated board rack. Each colour has its own slot — do not stack colours together or store boards flat on surfaces where they can be grabbed at random. The visual order of the rack is your first daily check that all boards are present and accounted for.
