Color Coding System

Role: All Kitchen Staff Updated: May 2026 Category: Hygiene

Color coding prevents cross-contamination at the source. Every cutting board and handled utensil in this kitchen is color-coded. Using the wrong color transfers harmful bacteria from raw protein onto ready-to-eat food, even after washing. Know your colors — use them every time.

Color Reference Chart
Color
Use For
■ Red
Raw red meat (beef, lamb, pork, venison)
■ Yellow
Raw poultry (chicken, duck, turkey)
■ Blue
Raw fish and seafood
■ Green
Fresh produce, fruit, salad, and vegetables
■ White
Dairy, bread, pastry, and cooked ready-to-eat foods
■ Purple / Brown
Allergen-dedicated prep (no cross-contact)

Rule 1

One color per task — never swap mid-prep

If you start a task on a red board, complete that task on the red board before switching. Do not move an item from a red board to a white board — the contamination moves with it. If you need to switch food categories mid-prep, swap boards completely and wash your hands and tools before continuing.

Note — This applies to utensils too. The tongs used to handle raw chicken must not be used to plate cooked chicken. Use a fresh set of tongs or wash and sanitize between uses.

Rule 2

Fridge storage follows the same color hierarchy

The color coding system mirrors the fridge storage order. Raw proteins are always stored below ready-to-eat foods — exactly as the colors suggest. In the fridge: ready-to-eat foods, dairy, cooked foods, and washed produce on the top shelf; raw fish and seafood below; whole cuts of raw beef, pork, and lamb below that; ground meat and ground seafood below whole cuts; and raw poultry on the bottom shelf. If your fridge has dedicated zones, keep proteins in their labelled zone only.

Shelf position
What goes here
Top shelf
Ready-to-eat foods, dairy, cooked foods, washed produce (White)
Middle shelf
Raw fish / seafood (Blue)
Lower middle shelf
Whole cuts of raw beef, pork, lamb (Red)
Lower shelf
Ground meat / ground seafood (Orange or Brown)
Bottom shelf
Raw poultry: chicken, turkey, duck (Yellow)

Note — All raw proteins must be stored covered and in sealed containers. Never store unwrapped raw protein directly on a shelf where drip can reach food below — even if the shelf order is correct.

Rule 3

Wash and sanitize boards between uses — not just between colors

Before switching a board from one food to another within the same color category (e.g., two different raw beef items), wash it with hot soapy water, rinse, and sanitize with the correct sanitizer solution. A board that looks clean is not necessarily sanitized. The color-coding system reduces risk — it does not eliminate the need to clean between uses.

Note — Report damaged boards to your manager immediately. Boards with deep score marks, warping, or discoloration harbour bacteria in the grooves and cannot be effectively sanitized. They must be replaced.

Rule 4

Store boards in designated positions only

At the end of each shift, boards are washed, sanitized, and stored vertically in the designated board rack. Each color has its own slot — do not stack colors together or store boards flat on surfaces where they can be grabbed at random. The visual order of the rack is your first daily check that all boards are present and accounted for.
