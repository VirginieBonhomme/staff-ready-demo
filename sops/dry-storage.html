Storage · Back of House

Dry Storage

Department / Area Back of House · Storage
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Dry Storage defines the required standard, sequence, and controls for this procedure.

Key Actions

  • Store nothing on the floor — 15 cm minimum clearance.
  • Label everything that leaves its original packaging.
  • Rotate stock — first in, first out.
1

Purpose

This SOP defines the required standard for dry storage so the team follows the same safe, consistent process every shift.

2

Scope

Applies to All Kitchen Staff and any team member assigned to this procedure, related checks, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Chef, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition

Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.

5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Approved tools, equipment, utensils, and supplies required for the task.
  • Required personal protective equipment, cleaning supplies, labels, logs, or checklists.
  • Working hand sink, sanitizer, thermometer, or other food safety control equipment when applicable.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

Step 1 - Store nothing on the floor — 15 cm minimum clearance

All dry goods must be stored on shelving at least 15 centimetres (6 inches) off the floor. This is a legal requirement, not a tidiness preference. It allows cleaning access underneath and protects stock from pest activity and moisture. Any items found on the floor during an opening check go on the shelf immediately.

Note — Items on the floor is one of the top triggers for a failed environmental health inspection. Zero exceptions.

Step 2 - Label everything that leaves its original packaging

Any dry ingredient decanted into a container — flour, sugar, spices, dried pulses — must be labelled with the product name and date decanted. Use waterproof labels. This applies even if the container has a clear lid and the contents look obvious. Over a busy service, assumptions cause mistakes.

Step 3 - Rotate stock — first in, first out

When restocking dry goods from a delivery, move older stock to the front of the shelf before placing new items behind it. Check the use-by or best-before date on both old and new stock. If new stock has an earlier use-by than existing stock, place it in front. This is FIFO — First In, First Out — and it applies to every shelf, every time.

Note — Write the delivery date on bulk items with a marker when they arrive. It makes rotation faster and error-free.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Corrective Action

If the standard cannot be met

Stop the task, tell the manager, isolate any affected food, equipment, or records, and follow the corrective action process before resuming.

If food safety may be affected

Do not serve or store the affected item until a manager confirms the next action. Discard when safety cannot be verified.

8

Quality Control / Verification

Follow Sequence Complete steps in order
Stop If Unsure Ask the manager before guessing
Keep Clean Reset tools and surfaces after use
9

Documentation & Records

Record any failed check, corrective action, manager decision, or required sign-off before the end of the shift.
