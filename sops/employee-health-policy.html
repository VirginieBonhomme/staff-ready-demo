Employee Health Policy

Role: All Staff Updated: May 2026 Category: Hygiene

This policy protects guests and colleagues. If you are ill and handle food, you risk causing a foodborne outbreak. No shift is worth that. Report symptoms to your manager before you enter the kitchen — not after you arrive.

Exclusion — Stay Home

You must not come to work if you have any of the following

Call your manager as early as possible — ideally two hours before your shift — if you are experiencing any of the symptoms below. Do not come in and then report it at the kitchen door.

  • Vomiting or diarrhoea (or both) in the last 48 hours
  • Jaundice (yellowing of the skin or eyes)
  • Diagnosed or suspected Norovirus, Salmonella, E. coli O157, Shigella, or Hepatitis A
  • Persistent unexplained high fever

Note — You must be symptom-free for at least 48 hours after the last episode of vomiting or diarrhoea before returning to work. A negative GP visit alone is not sufficient — 48 hours symptom-free is the minimum.

Restriction — Inform Manager

You must report these symptoms before your shift starts

The following conditions do not automatically exclude you from work but require manager assessment. You may be moved to a non-food-handling role, or sent home depending on severity.

  • Sore throat accompanied by a fever
  • An infected wound, boil, or skin lesion on the hands, wrists, or face
  • Persistent sneezing or runny nose with direct food contact risk
  • Persistent cough that cannot be controlled

If you have an infected cut or wound on your hands or wrists, it must be covered with a blue detectable plaster before you handle food, and gloves must be worn over it. Open wounds that cannot be fully covered must be excluded from food handling duties.

Note — Managers must document the restriction decision, the date, and any action taken. This record must be kept on file.

Return to Work

Returning after illness

Staff returning after a gastrointestinal illness (vomiting or diarrhoea) must confirm they have been symptom-free for at least 48 hours before their first shift back. For diagnosed Norovirus, Hepatitis A, or other notifiable illness, clearance from a GP or public health authority may be required before return. Your manager will advise.

On return, you are required to:

  • Confirm symptom-free status with your manager before starting work
  • Wash hands more frequently for the first day back
  • Report immediately if symptoms return during a shift

Note — Managers: record the date of return and confirmation of symptom-free status in the staff health log.

Reporting During a Shift

If you become ill mid-shift

If you begin experiencing vomiting, diarrhoea, or other symptoms listed above during your shift, stop food handling immediately and inform your manager. Do not continue working and do not attempt to finish the current service. Leave the food handling area, wash your hands, and wait for your manager to advise next steps.

Any food you were preparing or handling at the time of symptom onset must be assessed by the manager and discarded if there is any risk of contamination.

Note — There is no penalty for reporting illness. Trying to work through it is the greater risk to the business, your colleagues, and your guests.
