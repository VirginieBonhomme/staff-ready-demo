Staff Ready Ops. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
SOP Example

Hygiene · All Staff

Employee Health Policy

Department / Area All Departments · Hygiene
Prepared By Manager, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Report illness before entering food-handling areas so guests, coworkers, and the business are protected from foodborne illness risk.

Key Actions

  • Exclude staff with reportable symptoms.
  • Cover wounds with approved waterproof dressings.
  • Send staff home if symptoms start mid-shift.
  • Report illness after food handling immediately.
  • Keep excluded staff out of food areas.
1

Purpose

This SOP explains when staff must stay home, when symptoms must be reported to a manager, and what is required before returning to work after illness.

2

Scope

Applies to all staff, managers, contractors, and anyone entering kitchen, prep, dish, storage, service, or guest-facing work areas.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.
Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.
Corrective ActionThe required response when a step, limit, record, or safety standard is missed or cannot be verified.
5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Approved tools, equipment, utensils, and supplies required for the task.
  • Required personal protective equipment, cleaning supplies, labels, logs, or checklists.
  • Working hand sink, sanitizer, thermometer, or other food safety control equipment when applicable.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

Exclusion

Do not come to work if you have any of the following. Call your manager as early as possible before your shift.

  • Vomiting or diarrhoea in the last 48 hours
  • Jaundice, including yellowing of the skin or eyes
  • Diagnosed or suspected Norovirus, Salmonella, E. coli O157, Shigella, or Hepatitis A
  • Persistent unexplained high fever

Return Limit

You must be symptom-free for at least 48 hours after the last episode of vomiting or diarrhoea before returning to work.

Restriction

Report these symptoms before the shift starts. A manager will decide whether you can work, need restricted duties, or must go home.

  • Sore throat with fever
  • Infected wound, boil, or skin lesion on hands, wrists, or face
  • Persistent sneezing or runny nose with direct food contact risk
  • Persistent cough that cannot be controlled

Wounds

Infected cuts or wounds on hands or wrists must be covered with a blue detectable plaster before food handling, with gloves worn over it. Open wounds that cannot be fully covered are excluded from food handling.

Reporting Procedure

Before Shift Call the manager before arriving
At Work Stop food handling immediately
Manager Record decision and action taken

If symptoms start mid-shift

Stop food handling immediately, inform your manager, leave the food-handling area, wash your hands, and wait for manager direction. Do not try to finish the current service.

Return to Work

Staff returning after gastrointestinal illness must confirm they have been symptom-free for at least 48 hours before starting their first shift back.

  • Confirm symptom-free status with the manager before starting work
  • Wash hands more frequently for the first day back
  • Report immediately if symptoms return during a shift
  • Follow any GP or public health authority clearance requirements for diagnosed or notifiable illness
7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Corrective Action

If illness was reported after food handling

The manager must assess any food, utensils, boards, surfaces, or equipment handled by the staff member and discard or sanitize anything that may be contaminated.

If a staff member works while excluded

Remove the staff member from food-handling duties immediately, document the incident, assess affected food and surfaces, and follow the corrective action process.

Records

Managers must record restriction decisions, return-to-work confirmation, and any corrective action in the staff health log.

8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Complete any required checklist, log, label, or manager note tied to this SOP.
  • Record corrective actions with the date, time, item or area affected, action taken, and manager initials.
  • Keep completed records in the assigned location for manager review and inspection readiness.
