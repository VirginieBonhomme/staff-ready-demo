Pest Control

Role: All Staff / Manager Updated: May 2026 Category: Hygiene

Evidence of pests is an immediate fail during a health inspection and can result in closure. All staff must know the signs of pest activity and report any sighting or evidence to a manager immediately — the same day, not at the next team meeting.

Step 1

Know what to look for — signs of pest activity

Pest evidence is not always a live sighting. Often the signs appear before you see the pest itself. During your opening checks and cleaning, look actively for the following:

  • Rodents (mice/rats): Droppings (small dark pellets near walls, under equipment, in dry stores), gnaw marks on packaging, grease trails along walls or pipes, nesting material (shredded paper or fabric), strong ammonia-like odour
  • Cockroaches: Egg cases (small dark oval casings), shed skins, small brown smear marks near cracks and crevices, live sightings at night or when lights are turned on suddenly
  • Flies: Larvae (maggots) near drains, waste areas, or decaying food, fly specks (small black dots on surfaces), live flies in the kitchen during closed hours
  • Stored product insects: Beetles, weevils, or moths in dry goods such as flour, rice, or nuts — often identified by webbing, damage to packaging, or visible insects in the product

Step 2

Report immediately and isolate affected areas or products

If you find evidence of pests, report it to your manager immediately. Do not clean it up before the manager has seen it — the location and extent of the evidence matters for identifying the access point. If pest activity is found in a food storage area, isolate any food that may have been contaminated and do not use it until the manager and your pest control contractor have assessed.

Note — Take a photo of the evidence if possible before cleaning. This helps the pest contractor identify species and likely entry points, and provides a record for your due diligence log.

Step 3

Work with your licensed pest control contractor

All pest treatments must be carried out by a licensed pest control contractor — do not attempt DIY treatments in food preparation areas. Your contractor should visit on a scheduled basis (typically monthly or quarterly depending on risk level) and provide a written report after each visit. The report must record: date, areas inspected, evidence found, treatments applied, recommendations made, and next visit date.

Pest control reports must be filed and retained for a minimum of 12 months. They are frequently requested by environmental health officers during inspections.

Note — Keep the pest contractor's emergency contact number posted in the manager's office and in the kitchen manager's phone.

Prevention

Daily prevention practices that close pest access points

Effective pest control is mostly prevention. The following daily practices remove the conditions pests need to survive and establish:

  • Remove all waste to the external bin store before closing and ensure bin lids are sealed
  • Clean floor drains daily — blocked or dirty drains are a breeding ground for flies and cockroaches
  • Remove all food debris from under and behind equipment during daily cleaning
  • Store all dry goods in sealed containers off the floor — never in opened supplier bags left on shelving
  • Check deliveries for pest activity before accepting (see Receiving & Inspection SOP)
  • Report any structural defects — gaps around pipes, broken door seals, missing drain covers — to management immediately
  • Ensure external doors are kept closed when not in active use during service
