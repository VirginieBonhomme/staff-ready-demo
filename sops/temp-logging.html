Food Safety · Back of House

Walk-in & Freezer Temperature Logging

Department / Area Back of House · Food Safety
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Keep every cold unit in the safe range so food does not sit in the temperature danger zone, and so the restaurant has a written record for managers, audits, and health inspections.

Key Actions

  • Check the fridge display first thing on arrival.
  • Probe-check a mid-shelf item to verify.
  • Record the check before the end of the shift.
1

Purpose

This SOP explains how to check, verify, and record walk-in cooler, reach-in fridge, and freezer temperatures at the start and end of each operating day.

2

Scope

Applies to every walk-in cooler, reach-in fridge, lowboy, prep fridge, and freezer used by the kitchen. Opening shift logs the first readings. Closing shift logs the final readings before lock-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Chef, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.
Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.
Corrective ActionThe required response when a step, limit, record, or safety standard is missed or cannot be verified.
5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Calibrated probe thermometer
  • Sanitizer wipes or approved probe sanitizer
  • Kitchen tablet, phone, or printed daily temperature log
  • Manager contact if any reading is outside the safe range
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

Safe Ranges

Cold Holding 41°F or below
Frozen Storage 0°F or below
Danger Zone 41°F to 140°F

Critical Limit

Food must never sit in the danger zone for more than 2 hours. Any failed reading must be logged with the corrective action taken.

Step 1

Check the fridge display first thing on arrival

Before opening the fridge or handling food, check the temperature display. The reading must show 41°F or below. Record the temperature, time, unit name, and your initials immediately.

Step 2

Probe-check a mid-shelf item to verify

Use a sanitized probe thermometer to check the center of a dense, covered ready-to-eat item on the middle shelf. The center reading must be 41°F or below. Sanitize the probe before and after use.

Step 3

Check and log freezer temperature

Check each freezer display and record the reading alongside the fridge readings. Freezers must read 0°F or below. If a freezer is warmer than 5°F, notify the manager during opening.

Step 4

Submit the daily temperature log

Enter all readings into the Daily Temperature Log or the opening checklist form before service starts. Paper notes are not the official record unless a manager has approved them during a system outage.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Corrective Action

If a fridge reads above 41°F

Keep the door closed, notify the manager, and probe-check food as directed. Do not use food from the unit until a manager confirms it is safe.

If a fridge reads above 46°F

Stop using the unit immediately. Do not open the door again unless instructed by the manager. Log the failed reading and follow the Corrective Action SOP.

If food is out of temperature

Record the item, temperature, time found, and action taken. Discard food when required. Never relabel, cool, or serve food that cannot be proven safe.

8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

Temperature logs must be completed every day the kitchen is open and retained for a minimum of 3 months. Managers review missed logs, failed readings, and corrective actions during shift close and inspection prep.
