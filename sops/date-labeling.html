Storage · Back of House

Date Labelling & Food Rotation

Department / Area Back of House · Storage
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Unlabelled food is a fail during any health inspection. Every item in the fridge, freezer, and dry store that has been opened, prepped, or portioned must carry a label showing what it is, when it was made or opened, and when it must be used by. No exceptions.

Key Actions

  • Label every prepped item at the time of preparation.
  • Apply the correct shelf-life by food type.
  • Rotate stock using FIFO — First In, First Out.
  • Check dates at the start of every shift.
1

Purpose

This SOP defines the required standard for date labelling & food rotation so the team follows the same safe, consistent process every shift.

2

Scope

Applies to All Kitchen Staff and any team member assigned to this procedure, related checks, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Chef, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

Note — These are maximum safe storage times. Sensory checks (smell, color, texture) should still be applied on each use, regardless of the use-by date.

Step 1 - Label every prepped item at the time of preparation

The label goes on before the container goes into storage — not later, not at the end of the shift. Labels must be written clearly in permanent marker on blue food-safe masking tape or printed adhesive labels. If you cannot read the label from arm's length, rewrite it. Every label must include three pieces of information: the item name, the date prepared, and the use-by date.

Note — "Sauce" is not sufficient. Write the full name: "Chicken Jus" or "Carrot Puree." This matters during allergen checks and stock rotation.

Step 2 - Apply the correct shelf-life by food type

The use-by date depends on what the food is and how it was prepared. Use the table below as your reference. When in doubt, apply the shorter shelf life. If a recipe sheet specifies a different shelf life, follow the recipe — recipe-level specs take priority over general rules.

Step 3 - Rotate stock using FIFO — First In, First Out

When placing new prep or new deliveries into storage, always put the newer stock behind or below the older stock. The oldest item with the earliest use-by date comes out first. This is FIFO — First In, First Out. Do not push older stock to the back when loading new product. If you find two containers of the same item, the one with the earlier use-by date goes at the front.

Step 4 - Check dates at the start of every shift

Part of the opening check is a date audit — pull every labelled item and confirm it is within date before service starts. Any item at or past its use-by date must be discarded immediately and logged. Do not hold it "just in case" or use it in staff meals. The discard must be noted in the waste log.

Note — If you find unlabelled food during an opening check, do not assume it is safe. Discard it, and raise the issue with the prep team to prevent it happening again.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Corrective Action

If the standard cannot be met

Stop the task, tell the manager, isolate any affected food, equipment, or records, and follow the corrective action process before resuming.

If food safety may be affected

Do not serve or store the affected item until a manager confirms the next action. Discard when safety cannot be verified.

8

Quality Control / Verification

Stop If Unsure Ask the manager before guessing
Keep Clean Reset tools and surfaces after use
9

Documentation & Records

Record any failed check, corrective action, manager decision, or required sign-off before the end of the shift.
