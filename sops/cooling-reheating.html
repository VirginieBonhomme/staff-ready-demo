Food Safety · Back of House

Cooling & Reheating

Department / Area Back of House · Food Safety
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Improper cooling is the leading cause of foodborne illness in commercial kitchens. Hot food left to cool slowly passes through the danger zone (41°F–140°F) for too long, allowing bacteria to multiply rapidly. Cool fast, label always, reheat to piping hot — every time.

1

Purpose

This SOP defines the required standard for cooling & reheating so the team follows the same safe, consistent process every shift.

2

Scope

Applies to Kitchen Staff and any team member assigned to this procedure, related checks, or manager follow-up.

3

4

Procedure

Step 1 - Divide into shallow portions immediately

As soon as a batch of food finishes cooking, divide it into shallow containers no deeper than 7 cm. Large, deep containers retain heat in the centre and will not cool fast enough. Transfer to gastronorm trays, deli containers, or hotel pans — do not leave food cooling in the pot it was cooked in.

Note — Larger joints or whole proteins should be portioned or sliced before cooling. The goal is to maximise surface area so heat escapes quickly.

Step 2 - Cool using an ice bath or blast chiller

Place shallow containers into an ice bath — a larger container or the prep sink filled with ice and cold water. Stir the food regularly to release heat from the centre. Alternatively use the blast chiller if available; set to blast-chill mode and load uncovered trays in a single layer. The goal is to bring food below 70°F within 2 hours.

Note — Do not place hot food directly into the fridge. It raises the fridge temperature and puts other food at risk. Always ice-bath first.

Step 3 - Check temperature and refrigerate within 90 minutes

Probe the centre of the food regularly during cooling. Once the food reads below 70°F — ideally within 90 minutes of coming off heat — transfer to the fridge. Continue monitoring until the core temperature reaches 41°F or below. This must happen within a total of 4 hours from when cooling began.

Note — Sanitize the probe before and after every reading. Cross-contaminating between products during cooling is a common oversight.

Step 4 - Label with date, time, and use-by

Once the food is at or below 41°F, cover, label, and store correctly. Every container must show: item name, date prepared, time cooled, and use-by date. Cooked foods held at 41°F or below should be used within 3 days unless the recipe specifies otherwise. See the Date Labelling SOP for full rules.

Note — If food has not reached 41°F within 4 hours total from the start of cooling, discard it. Do not attempt to re-cool or reheat. Log the discard and notify your manager.

Step 5 - Reheat rapidly to 165°F throughout

Food being reheated for service must reach a core temperature of 165°F throughout — probe the thickest or densest part of the item. Reheating should be done quickly, ideally on a direct heat source (oven, stove, regen oven). Do not reheat in a hot-hold unit — hot-hold is not designed to bring cold food up to temperature, only to maintain food that is already hot.

Note — Food must be reheated once only. If a reheated item is not used, it must be discarded — not cooled and reheated again.

Step 6 - Log the reheat temperature before service

Record the reheated food item, the time, the core temperature achieved, and your initials in the daily temperature log before placing the food into service or hot-hold. If the food did not reach 165°F, remove it from service, reheat again, and re-probe. If it fails again, discard and log the corrective action.

Note — See the Daily Temperature Log for the form and the Corrective Action SOP for what to do when a reheat fails.

6

Corrective Action

If the standard cannot be met

Stop the task, tell the manager, isolate any affected food, equipment, or records, and follow the corrective action process before resuming.

If food safety may be affected

Do not serve or store the affected item until a manager confirms the next action. Discard when safety cannot be verified.

7

Records

Record any failed check, corrective action, manager decision, or required sign-off before the end of the shift.
