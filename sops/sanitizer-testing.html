Hygiene · Back of House

Sanitizer Concentration Testing

Department / Area Back of House · Hygiene
Prepared By Manager, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Sanitizer solution that is too weak does not kill pathogens. Solution that is too strong is a chemical hazard. Test every fresh batch of sanitizer solution with a test strip before use. Do not guess — the color does not change at the wrong concentration.

1

Purpose

This SOP defines the required standard for sanitizer concentration testing so the team follows the same safe, consistent process every shift.

2

Scope

Applies to All Kitchen Staff and any team member assigned to this procedure, related checks, or manager follow-up.

3

Reference

Concentration Reference

Sanitizer Type
Correct Range
Chlorine (bleach-based)
100–200 ppm
Quaternary ammonium (quat)
200–400 ppm
Below minimum
Discard and remake
Above maximum
Discard and remake

When to Test

At the start of every shift — when making fresh sanitizer solution
Every 2 hours during service — three-sink sanitize compartment
Whenever a new batch of solution is made mid-shift
If solution looks cloudy, discolored, or has visible food debris
When testing a new product or supplier change
4

Procedure

Step 1 - Mix the sanitizer solution correctly

Always add the sanitizer chemical to water — never add water to concentrated chemical. Follow the dilution ratio on the product label exactly. Use cold or lukewarm water for chlorine-based sanitizers (hot water breaks down chlorine and reduces effectiveness). Use the measuring markings on your container or a measured dispenser pump. Do not free-pour.

Note — Do not mix sanitizer products together. Do not add more chemical to an existing solution to "top it up." If a solution is depleted or contaminated, discard it and make a fresh batch.

Step 2 - Dip the correct test strip and read immediately

Dip the test strip into the sanitizer solution for one second, then remove and hold flat. Read the color change within 10 seconds — do not wait longer, as the strip will continue to change and give a false reading. Compare the strip to the color chart on the packaging. Make sure you are using the correct test strip for your sanitizer type — chlorine strips do not work for quat solutions and vice versa.

Step 3 - Act on the result — pass, adjust, or discard

If the strip reads within the correct range, the solution is ready to use. If the reading is below the minimum, the solution is too weak — discard it and make a fresh batch with the correct dilution. If the reading is above the maximum, the solution is too strong — discard it, do not attempt to dilute by adding more water to an existing over-concentrated solution. Log the result either way.

Note — Sanitizer solutions weaken over time, with heavy use, and when contaminated with food debris. Always change the solution in the three-sink sanitize compartment at least every 2 hours during service, and always make a fresh batch at the start of each shift.

Step 4 - Log the result in the sanitizer log

Record every test on the Daily Sanitizer Log: date, time, sanitizer type, ppm reading, pass or fail, and action taken if failed. This log is a required record under food safety due diligence and must be retained for a minimum of 3 months.

5

Standards

Follow Sequence Complete steps in order
Stop If Unsure Ask the manager before guessing
Keep Clean Reset tools and surfaces after use
6

Corrective Action

If the standard cannot be met

Stop the task, tell the manager, isolate any affected food, equipment, or records, and follow the corrective action process before resuming.

If food safety may be affected

Do not serve or store the affected item until a manager confirms the next action. Discard when safety cannot be verified.

7

Records

Record any failed check, corrective action, manager decision, or required sign-off before the end of the shift.
