Staff Ready Ops. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
SOP Example

Food Safety · Back of House

Corrective Action

Department / Area Back of House · Food Safety
Prepared By Chef, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

When a food safety control fails, stop the affected food or process, assess the risk, fix the root cause, and document the action before the end of the shift.

Key Actions

  • Stop and isolate.
  • Assess the risk.
  • Fix the root cause.
  • Record the action before the end of the shift.
1

Purpose

This SOP explains what to do when a food safety control measure fails, including temperature, sanitizer, labelling, storage, allergen, illness, pest, or equipment controls.

2

Scope

Applies to all kitchen staff, managers, and any team member who identifies a failed food safety control during receiving, prep, service, closing, cleaning, or storage.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Chef, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.
Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.
Corrective ActionThe required response when a step, limit, record, or safety standard is missed or cannot be verified.
HACCPHazard Analysis and Critical Control Point; a written food safety plan required for certain specialized processes.
5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Approved tools, equipment, utensils, and supplies required for the task.
  • Required personal protective equipment, cleaning supplies, labels, logs, or checklists.
  • Working hand sink, sanitizer, thermometer, or other food safety control equipment when applicable.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

Common Triggers

  • Fridge or freezer temperature out of safe range
  • Food not cooled to 41°F within the required time
  • Reheated food not reaching 165°F
  • Sanitizer concentration below or above required range
  • Unlabelled or out-of-date food found in storage
  • Raw protein stored above ready-to-eat food
  • Allergen-modified dish prepared without clean board or tools
  • Sick staff member reporting for a shift while excluded
  • Pest evidence found in food storage or preparation areas

Critical Rule

Do not ignore the failure and continue service. Stop, isolate, assess, act, and document.

Step 1 — Stop and isolate

Stop using the affected food, equipment, or process immediately. Move food off the line or away from prep and label it "ON HOLD - DO NOT USE" until a manager has assessed it.

Step 2 — Assess the risk

A manager must assess how long the control failed, what temperature was reached, what food was affected, and whether the food can be safely used, reworked, returned, or discarded.

Step 3 — Fix the root cause

Correct the process or equipment issue before resuming. Repair faulty equipment, remake sanitizer, retrain the team member, adjust prep timing, or change storage as needed.

Step 4 — Document the action

Complete the corrective action log before the end of the shift. Include what failed, what food or process was affected, action taken, root cause, who was notified, and initials.

Decision Guide

Decision
When to Use
Discard
Food was in the danger zone for more than 2 hours, contaminated, untraceable, or the failure cannot be verified
Corrective cook / reheat
Undercooked food can be immediately cooked or reheated to the safe required temperature and re-probed
Use immediately
Failure was caught early and manager confirms food stayed within safe limits
Return to supplier
Received goods failed delivery inspection and must not enter stock

Manager Review

Managers review corrective action records during close, weekly food safety checks, and inspection prep. Repeated failures must trigger retraining, equipment repair, process changes, or escalation to ownership.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Follow all food safety, workplace safety, sanitation, allergen, chemical, equipment, and regulatory requirements that apply to this task. Stop and notify the manager when safety cannot be verified.

8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

Record Same Day Before shift end
Keep With Temperature and sanitizer logs
Retain For Minimum 3 months

Required Details

Date, time, what failed, affected food or process, action taken, root cause, person notified, and initials must be included in the corrective action record.
