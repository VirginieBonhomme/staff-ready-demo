Corrective Action

Role: All Kitchen Staff / Manager Updated: May 2026 Category: Food Safety

A corrective action is required any time a food safety control measure fails. This includes: temperature out of range, sanitizer below correct strength, food found unlabelled, a food safety SOP not followed, or equipment failure during service. Do not ignore the failure and continue — stop, assess, act, and document.

Common Triggers
Fridge or freezer temperature out of safe range on opening check
Food has not cooled to 41°F within the required time
Reheated food did not reach 165°F
Sanitizer concentration below or above required range
Unlabelled or out-of-date food found in fridge or dry store
Raw protein stored above ready-to-eat food in fridge
Allergen-modified dish prepared without clean board/tools
Sick staff member who was not excluded reporting for shift
Pest evidence found in food storage or preparation area

Step 1

Stop — isolate the food or process immediately

As soon as you identify that a critical control has been breached, stop using the affected food or process. Do not continue service with food that may be unsafe. Remove the food from the service line or prep area and label it "ON HOLD — DO NOT USE" until a manager has assessed it. If equipment has failed, stop using it and notify the manager.

Note — "On hold" does not mean the food will be discarded. It means no decisions have been made yet. The manager will assess and decide.

Step 2

Assess — is the food safe or must it be discarded?

A manager must assess the food before any decision is made to use it, discard it, or rework it. The assessment considers: how long has the control been failed, what temperature was reached, what is the food type and its risk level, is there any visible evidence of spoilage. When in doubt, the manager must err on the side of discarding.

Common decisions:

  • Discard: Food was in the danger zone for more than 2 hours, or the extent of the failure cannot be determined
  • Continue with corrective cooking: Undercooked protein — reheat immediately to 165°F and re-probe
  • Use immediately: Fridge failure caught early with food still cold — use food that day if temperature has not exceeded safe range
  • Return to supplier: Received goods that failed delivery inspection

Step 3

Fix the root cause before resuming

Addressing the food is not enough on its own. Fix the process or equipment that caused the failure before resuming. If the fridge is warm, find out why and rectify it. If sanitizer was mixed incorrectly, retrain the team member. If a cooling step was skipped, identify why and adjust the prep schedule. If equipment is broken, arrange repair or replacement — do not continue working around it.

Note — A corrective action that only addresses the food and not the root cause will result in the same failure happening again. The documentation in Step 4 should include the root cause and what was done to prevent recurrence.

Step 4

Document everything — the same day

Complete a corrective action log entry before the end of the shift. The record must include: date and time of the failure, what was found, which food or process was affected, the action taken (discarded, reheated, returned), the root cause, who was notified, and your initials. Corrective action records are one of the key documents an environmental health officer will check during an inspection to verify your food safety management system is working.

Note — Corrective action logs must be retained for a minimum of 3 months. Keep them with your temperature logs and sanitizer logs in the food safety record file.
