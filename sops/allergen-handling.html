Allergen Handling

Role: All Kitchen Staff Updated: May 2026 Category: Allergens

The Big 9 allergens: Milk · Eggs · Fish · Shellfish · Tree Nuts · Peanuts · Wheat · Soybeans · Sesame. Any guest flagging an allergy must be taken seriously — every time, no exceptions.

Step 1

Receive the allergen ticket — stop and read it fully

When a ticket arrives with an allergen flag, stop what you are doing and read it in full before starting the dish. Confirm which allergen is flagged and identify every component of the dish that may contain or have contacted that allergen. If you are unsure about any ingredient, ask a supervisor — do not guess.

Note — An allergy is not a preference. If a guest has been told a dish is allergen-free and it isn't, the consequences can be life-threatening.

Step 2

Use clean boards, clean tools, clean gloves

Before preparing an allergen-modified dish, wash and sanitise all surfaces and switch to a clean board — do not use one that has already been used for service prep. Change to fresh gloves and wash hands first. Use utensils taken directly from clean storage, not ones sitting in a shared bain-marie.

Step 3

Plate separately and label clearly

Allergen-modified dishes must be plated on a clean plate that has not been used for any other food. Do not plate next to a dish containing the allergen and then remove it — contact has already occurred. Place a coloured allergen flag or toothpick in the dish and call out the allergen when passing to front of house.

Note — Say it out loud on the pass: "Table 4 — allergen dish, no gluten." Front of house confirms before it goes to the table.
