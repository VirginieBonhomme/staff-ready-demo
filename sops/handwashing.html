Hygiene · All Staff

Handwashing Procedure

Department / Area All Departments · Hygiene
Prepared By Manager, Kitchen Manager
Date Reviewed May 2026
Version 1.0

Wash hands at the right moments and with the correct technique so food, equipment, guests, and coworkers are protected from contamination.

Key Actions

  • Wet hands with warm running water.
  • Apply soap and lather for 20 seconds.
  • Rinse thoroughly.
  • Dry with a single-use paper towel.
1

Purpose

This SOP explains when staff must wash their hands and the exact method required at every designated handwashing sink.

2

Scope

Applies to all staff working in food preparation, dishwashing, service, storage, receiving, cleaning, and guest-facing areas. Gloves, utensils, and sanitizer do not replace handwashing.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager, Kitchen Manager) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.
Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.
Corrective ActionThe required response when a step, limit, record, or safety standard is missed or cannot be verified.
5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Designated handwashing sink with warm running water
  • Antibacterial hand soap
  • Single-use paper towels
  • Waste bin for used towels
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When to Wash

Wash hands every time one of these triggers happens, even if you washed recently.

  • Before starting work and at the start of every shift
  • Before handling ready-to-eat food
  • After handling raw meat, poultry, fish, or eggs in the shell
  • After touching your face, nose, mouth, hair, phone, tablet, or shared device
  • After blowing your nose, sneezing, coughing, eating, drinking, or taking a break
  • After removing gloves
  • After handling waste, bins, dirty dishes, money, receipts, or cleaning chemicals
  • After using the toilet
  • After touching any surface that may be contaminated, including door handles and equipment controls

Glove Rule

Wash hands before putting gloves on and after taking gloves off. Contaminated gloves can transfer contamination the same way bare hands can.

Step 1

Wet hands with warm running water

Use only the designated handwashing sink. Wet hands thoroughly with warm, comfortable running water. Remove jewellery from hands and wrists before beginning.

Step 2

Apply soap and lather for 20 seconds

Apply a full pump of antibacterial soap. Lather palms, backs of hands, between fingers, under nails, thumbs, and wrists for at least 20 seconds.

Step 3

Rinse thoroughly

Rinse under running water until all soap is removed. Hold hands angled downward so water runs off the fingertips instead of back toward the wrists.

Step 4

Dry with a single-use paper towel

Dry hands completely with a paper towel. Use the towel to turn off the tap if required, then dispose of it immediately. Cloth towels are not permitted.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Corrective Action

If the handwashing sink is blocked or unstocked

Stop food handling in that area and tell the manager immediately. Restock soap or paper towels before work continues.

If contamination may have occurred

Stop the task, wash hands correctly, change gloves if worn, replace contaminated utensils or boards, and discard any food that may have been touched with unwashed hands.

8

Quality Control / Verification

Minimum Time 20 seconds of lathering
Sink Use Handwashing sink only
Drying Paper towel only

Never Use

Do not wash hands in the prep sink, pot wash, bar sink, or dish area sink. Do not dry hands on aprons, cloth towels, uniforms, or shared cleaning cloths.

9

Documentation & Records

  • Complete any required checklist, log, label, or manager note tied to this SOP.
  • Record corrective actions with the date, time, item or area affected, action taken, and manager initials.
  • Keep completed records in the assigned location for manager review and inspection readiness.
