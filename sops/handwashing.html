Handwashing Procedure

Role: All Staff Updated: May 2026 Category: Hygiene

When to Wash

You must wash your hands in all of these situations — every time

Handwashing at the right moments is as important as handwashing correctly. Washing once at the start of your shift is not enough. You must wash your hands at every one of the triggers below, without exception.

  • Before starting work and at the start of every shift
  • Before handling any ready-to-eat food
  • After handling raw meat, poultry, or fish
  • After handling eggs in the shell
  • After touching your face, nose, mouth, or hair
  • After blowing your nose or sneezing
  • After removing gloves
  • After handling waste, bins, or rubbish bags
  • After touching a mobile phone, tablet, or shared device
  • After handling money or receipts
  • After cleaning or handling cleaning chemicals
  • After using the toilet
  • After a break — even a short one
  • After touching any surface that may be contaminated (door handles, equipment controls)

Note — Gloves do not replace handwashing. Wash hands before putting on gloves and after taking them off. Gloves that have touched a contaminated surface can transfer contamination just as bare hands can.

Step 1

Wet hands with warm running water

Turn on the tap and wet your hands thoroughly with warm — not hot — water. Water temperature should be comfortable to the touch. Remove any jewellery from hands and wrists before beginning.

Note — Only use the designated handwashing sink. Never the prep sink or pot wash.

Step 2

Apply soap and lather for 20 seconds

Apply a full pump of antibacterial soap from the dispenser. Work into a lather covering both palms, between fingers, under nails, and up to the wrists. Count to 20, or hum "Happy Birthday" twice at a steady pace — that's your minimum.

Note — 20 seconds minimum. Most people underestimate how long this is — count it out every time.

Step 3

Rinse thoroughly

Rinse hands under running water until all soap is removed. Hold hands angled downward so water runs off fingertips, not back up your wrists. Take your time — residual soap can cause skin irritation over a full shift.

Step 4

Dry with a single-use paper towel

Take one paper towel from the dispenser and dry hands completely. Use the paper towel to turn off the tap — do not touch the tap handle with clean bare hands. Dispose of the towel in the bin immediately.

Note — Cloth towels are not permitted for hand drying. Paper towel only, every time.
