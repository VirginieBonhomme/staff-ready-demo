Grill Cleaning

Role: Line Cook / Closer Updated: May 2026 Category: Equipment

Step 1

Scrape the grill while still hot — end of service

Immediately after the last ticket, while the grill is still at temperature, use the long-handled grill brush to scrape the grates from back to front. Work in firm parallel strokes — don't scrub back and forth. Debris falls into the drip tray below. Do not let the grill cool before scraping; baked-on carbon is far harder to remove.

Note — Use heat-resistant gloves. The grill surface will be 480°F or above at end of service.

Step 2

Turn off and allow to cool before deep clean

Switch the grill off at the control panel and allow it to cool to a safe handling temperature — below 140°F. This takes 20 to 30 minutes depending on the unit. Do not apply cleaning chemicals to a hot grill surface. Use this time to clean the surrounding station and empty the drip tray into the waste bin.

Step 3

Apply grill cleaner and scrub the grates

Spray grill cleaner across the grate surface and leave to dwell for the time specified on the bottle — typically 3 to 5 minutes. Using a grill stone or heavy-duty scourer, scrub grates in the direction of the bars. Rinse thoroughly with hot water. No chemical residue should remain on the cooking surface.

Note — Check the cleaner label. Some products require PPE (gloves, eye protection). Do not skip this.

Step 4

Season the grates before closing

Once the grates are clean, rinsed, and fully dry, apply a thin coat of vegetable oil with a folded cloth or paper. This protects the metal from rust overnight and improves food release at the next startup. Wipe off any excess — the surface should be lightly coated, not pooling with oil.
