Peaches Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-HEALTH-05

DOH-Informed · Staff Controls

DOH Employee Health & Hygiene

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Staff Controls
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

NYC DOH source rules for employee illness, handwashing, bare-hand contact, wounds, glove use, and hand sink access.

Key Actions

  • Do not allow food workers with listed restricted diseases, symptoms, or uncovered infected wounds to work in food operations.
  • Wash hands before food work and after every contamination trigger.
  • Prevent bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food.
  • Keep hand sinks within required access, stocked, unobstructed, and used only for handwashing.
1

Purpose

Prevent foodborne illness caused by sick workers, poor handwashing, infected wounds, bare-hand contact, and blocked or misused hand sinks.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Staff Controls and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.
Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.
Corrective ActionThe required response when a step, limit, record, or safety standard is missed or cannot be verified.
5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Do not allow a food worker to work if the worker has a food-related illness or a communicable disease listed in Article 81, including acute infectious diarrhea, amebiasis, cholera, cryptosporidiosis, diphtheria, E. coli O157:H7, giardiasis, hepatitis A, salmonellosis, shigellosis, streptococcal sore throat, tuberculosis, typhoid, or an infected cut or wound.
  • Workers must report vomiting, diarrhea, fever, jaundice, sore throat with fever, infected cuts/boils, diagnosed foodborne illness, or exposure that may create a food safety risk.
  • Hands and exposed arms must be washed before starting work and after toilet use, raw food handling, touching face/hair/body, coughing/sneezing, eating/drinking/smoking, handling garbage/chemicals, cleaning, using phones, handling money, changing tasks, or any possible contamination.
  • Ready-to-eat food must be prepared and served without bare-hand contact unless the food will be heated to the required Article 81 minimum temperature. Use gloves, deli tissue, tongs, spatulas, or other barriers.
  • Gloves are not a substitute for handwashing. Hands must be washed before putting on gloves and gloves must be changed whenever contaminated, torn, or when tasks change.
  • Handwashing sinks must be no more than 25 feet from food preparation, service, and warewashing areas, must be unobstructed, must have hot and cold running water, soap, drying supplies, and required signs, and must not be used for food prep, dumping, or warewashing.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • At pre-shift health screening or manager check-in.
  • When an employee reports illness during a shift.
  • Before any ready-to-eat food handling.
  • After any task change or contamination event.
  • During manager line checks for hand sink access and supplies.

Step 1 - Screen for illness

Managers check for visible illness and require staff to report vomiting, diarrhea, fever, jaundice, sore throat with fever, infected wounds, diagnosed foodborne illness, or exposure.

Step 2 - Exclude restricted workers

Do not allow food work when a worker has a restricted disease, high-risk symptom, or infected wound that cannot be safely covered. Escalate to the manager/PIC.

Step 3 - Keep handwashing available

Stock every hand sink with soap and drying supplies. Keep access clear and correct any sink used for storage, dumping, warewashing, or food prep.

Step 4 - Control ready-to-eat food

Use gloves or utensils for ready-to-eat food. Wash hands before gloves and change gloves whenever contaminated, torn, after interruptions, or when switching tasks.

Step 5 - Handle wounds correctly

Cover cuts or burns with a clean impermeable bandage. Use a glove over hand wounds. Exclude infected, draining, or uncovered wounds from food tasks.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Immediate Corrective Action

  • Remove ill or restricted workers from food operations immediately and document manager action.
  • Discard ready-to-eat food touched with bare hands or contaminated gloves unless it will receive required cooking.
  • Restock or unblock hand sinks immediately; pause prep in the affected area until handwashing is available.
  • If a worker handles food without washing after a contamination trigger, stop the task, discard affected food if needed, wash hands, change gloves, and sanitize surfaces.
  • Report suspected patron foodborne illness complaints or emergency occurrences to management immediately for required follow-up.

Inspection Notes

  • Article 81 covers food worker health and hygienic practices, handwashing, and bare-hand contact.
  • Self-inspection items include food worker illness, bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food, missing/blocked handwashing facilities, and improper hygienic practices.
  • Inspectors may observe staff handwashing behavior and ask where illness reporting is documented.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Employee illness report or manager incident note.
  • Corrective action log for bare-hand contact, hand sink failures, or contamination events.
  • Training record for handwashing, glove use, and employee health policy.
  • Patron illness complaint documentation and escalation notes.
