Good Ol Days Diner Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-COOL-02

DOH-Informed · Cooling Controls

DOH Cooling & Reheating

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Cooling Controls
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

Detailed two-stage cooling, approved cooling methods, reheating limits, and discard rules.

Key Actions

  • Cool from 140°F to 70°F within 2 hours.
  • Cool from 70°F to 41°F within 4 additional hours.
  • Reheat food for hot holding to 165°F within 2 hours.
  • Discard food when cooling or reheating limits cannot be proven.
1

Purpose

Control one of the highest-risk points in food service: cooked food passing slowly through the temperature danger zone during cooling or reheating.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Cooling Controls and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Two-stage cooling standard: 140°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then 70°F to 41°F within 4 additional hours. Total maximum cooling time is 6 hours.
  • Food made from ingredients at room temperature must be cooled to 41°F or below within 4 hours.
  • ROP foods have stricter cooling requirements and must meet the applicable 38°F and 34°F standards when used.
  • Reheat previously cooked and cooled food that requires time/temperature control for safety for hot holding to 165°F within 2 hours.
  • Never reheat in a steam table, soup warmer, or hot holding cabinet. Use a stove, oven, microwave, or other active cooking equipment.
  • Do not cover hot food tightly while cooling. Cover only after food reaches 41°F or below, unless loosely protected from overhead contamination.
  • Do not perform reduced oxygen packaging, cook-chill, or sous vide unless the restaurant has prior Department approval and an approved HACCP plan for that food/process.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • Any time cooked soups, sauces, rice, beans, meats, poultry, seafood, vegetables, or leftovers are cooled for later use.
  • Any time hot food is transferred to shallow pans, ice baths, blast chillers, or walk-ins.
  • Any time cooled food is reheated for service or hot holding.
  • Whenever a cooling log shows food is not dropping fast enough.

Step 1 - Reduce food depth and size

Move hot food into shallow pans, cut large pieces smaller, divide thick foods into smaller containers, and avoid deep covered containers.

Step 2 - Use an approved rapid-cooling method

Use ice baths with stirring, ice paddles, shallow pans, blast chilling, adding ice as an ingredient, or cold running water for appropriate foods.

Step 3 - Track the clock

Record start time and temperature, the 2-hour checkpoint, and the final 6-hour result. Do not estimate cooling from memory.

Step 4 - Reheat rapidly

Use active cooking equipment and stir as needed so the entire batch reaches 165°F within 2 hours before hot holding.

Step 5 - Verify before storage or service

Probe the center or thickest part. Label cooled food with name, date, and time once it reaches the required temperature.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Immediate Corrective Action

  • If food has not reached 70°F within 2 hours, reheat to 165°F and restart cooling, or discard.
  • If food has not reached 41°F within 6 total hours, discard.
  • If reheating will not reach 165°F within 2 hours, discard.
  • If a cooling method is failing, divide food into smaller portions, add ice bath/ice paddle support, move to a working cooler, and notify the manager.
  • Record discarded product and manager approval.

Inspection Notes

  • Self-inspection items include failure to cool 140°F to 70°F within 2 hours and 70°F to 41°F within 4 additional hours, and reheating failures to 165°F within 2 hours.
  • Inspectors may ask how thick foods are cooled and may probe food in walk-ins.
  • Covered deep containers of warm food are a high-risk inspection finding.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Cooling log with food name, start time/temp, 2-hour temp, final temp, corrective action, and initials.
  • Reheating log for reheated food held for service.
  • Corrective action log for missed cooling/reheating standards.
  • Date labels for cooled and stored food.
