Harbor & Hearth Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-ADV-09

DOH-Informed · Menu Advisory

DOH Consumer Advisory

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Menu Advisory
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

NYC DOH source requirements for written consumer advisories when raw or undercooked animal foods are served or offered cooked to order.

Key Actions

  • Use the required written advisory for raw or undercooked meats, poultry, seafood, shellfish, or eggs.
  • Mark or describe every menu item that is raw, undercooked, or cooked to order.
  • Do not serve raw or undercooked animal foods to higher-risk facilities or populations covered by Article 81 limits.
  • Review specials, brunch items, bar drinks, sauces, and seasonal menus before service.
1

Purpose

Make sure guests receive the required written warning when menu items contain or may be served as raw or undercooked animal foods.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Menu Advisory and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.
Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.
Corrective ActionThe required response when a step, limit, record, or safety standard is missed or cannot be verified.
5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • The written advisory text must state: “Consuming raw or undercooked meats, poultry, seafood, shellfish or eggs may increase your risk of foodborne illness.”
  • The advisory must appear on menus, menu boards, brochures, signage, table tents, placards, or other customer-facing menu materials where the food is offered.
  • Any food that needs the advisory must be marked with an asterisk or other mark, or described as raw, undercooked, or cooked to order.
  • The advisory applies to raw or undercooked meat, fish, molluscan shellfish, poultry, unpasteurized raw eggs, and foods containing those ingredients, including sauces, dressings, drinks, desserts, ceviche, sushi/sashimi, oysters, steak tartare, rare duck, and burgers or eggs cooked to order.
  • Raw or undercooked meat, eggs, fish, or molluscan shellfish must not be served in facilities that primarily serve higher-risk persons, including immunocompromised persons, senior centers, charitable feeding programs, custodial care facilities, hospitals, adult day care, assisted living, child care, camps, preschools, and elementary/secondary schools.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • Before printing or publishing any menu.
  • Before running specials that include raw, undercooked, or cooked-to-order animal foods.
  • Before offering brunch egg items, burgers cooked to order, raw bar items, sushi, ceviche, Caesar dressing, dessert mousse, or cocktails with raw egg.
  • Whenever a recipe changes to include unpasteurized raw egg or another raw/undercooked animal ingredient.

Step 1 - Identify affected items

Review every menu, special, bar drink, sauce, dressing, dessert, and verbal offer for raw, undercooked, or cooked-to-order animal foods.

Step 2 - Add item-level marking or description

Use an asterisk or clear description so the guest can identify which item is raw, undercooked, or cooked to order.

Step 3 - Place the advisory where guests see it

Put the written advisory on the menu, board, sign, placard, or other material used to sell the item.

Step 4 - Review restricted-service settings

If the operation serves a higher-risk facility or population, remove raw or undercooked animal-food service unless the legal requirement is clearly satisfied.

Step 5 - Train staff on guest questions

Staff must not tell guests an undercooked item is risk-free. Escalate questions about safety, allergies, pregnancy, immunocompromised status, or children to a manager.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Immediate Corrective Action

  • If a menu item requiring an advisory is being sold without one, stop selling the item until the advisory and item marking/description are corrected.
  • If a special is missing the advisory, update the printed special, chalkboard, QR menu, table tent, or server script before taking more orders.
  • If a guest asks whether an undercooked item is safe for a higher-risk person, do not reassure; involve a manager and offer a fully cooked alternative.
  • Document menu corrections and notify managers, FOH, bar, and kitchen of the change.

Inspection Notes

  • Inspectors may review menus, specials boards, QR menus, and table tents for the required advisory and item marking.
  • Common misses include brunch eggs, Caesar dressing, mousse, cocktails with raw egg, oysters, ceviche, sushi, and burgers listed as cooked to order.
  • The advisory does not replace required cooking temperatures; it applies only where Article 81 allows the item to be offered raw, undercooked, or cooked to order.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Current menu copy with advisory language.
  • Specials/menu change approval record.
  • Manager review checklist for raw/undercooked items.
  • Training note for FOH, bar, and expo staff.
