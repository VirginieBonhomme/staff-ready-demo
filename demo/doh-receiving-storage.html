Peaches Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-RECV-06

DOH-Informed · Source & Storage

DOH Receiving & Storage

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Source & Storage
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

Approved source, receiving temperatures, rejection rules, shellfish tags, storage order, labeling, FIFO, and dry storage controls.

Key Actions

  • Receive food only from approved sources.
  • Reject unsafe, damaged, unlabeled, pest-contaminated, or temperature-abused deliveries.
  • Keep shellfish tags for 90 days.
  • Store food covered, labeled, off the floor, and protected from cross-contamination.
1

Purpose

Stop unsafe food from entering the restaurant and protect accepted food during storage, rotation, and prep staging.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Source & Storage and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.

5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Cold food that requires time/temperature control for safety at receiving: 41°F or below unless a specific food has a different standard.
  • Smoked fish and applicable ROP foods: 38°F or below.
  • Raw shell eggs: ambient delivery/storage target of 45°F or below.
  • Frozen food: frozen solid, no large ice crystals, thawing, or refrozen appearance.
  • Hot delivered food: 140°F or above.
  • Shellfish must come from approved sources with shellfish tags; tags must be retained for 90 days.
  • Meat and poultry must show inspection/approved source documentation where applicable.
  • Dry storage should be clean, dry, ventilated, pest-free, and generally maintained around 70°F with about 50% humidity when possible.
  • Food must be stored at least 6 inches off the floor and protected from splash, dust, chemicals, and pests.
  • Do not perform reduced oxygen packaging, cook-chill, or sous vide unless the restaurant has prior Department approval and an approved HACCP plan for that food/process.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • At every delivery before the invoice is accepted.
  • When food is moved from receiving into walk-ins, freezers, dry storage, or prep areas.
  • During daily manager storage checks.
  • When damaged packaging, pest evidence, odor, temperature abuse, or missing labels are found.

Step 1 - Inspect the truck and delivery condition

Look for cleanliness, temperature control, pest evidence, chemical contamination, damaged boxes, leaks, odors, and signs of thaw/refreeze.

Step 2 - Verify source and labels

Confirm supplier, product identity, dates, shellfish tags, inspection marks, and allergen/ingredient labels where needed.

Step 3 - Take product temperatures

Probe or otherwise verify representative items that require time/temperature control for safety before acceptance. Check the warmest or highest-risk items first.

Step 4 - Reject before storing

Reject food that is out of temperature, spoiled, damaged, unlabeled, from unapproved sources, contaminated, or otherwise questionable.

Step 5 - Store by risk and rotation

Label and date items, use FIFO, store raw poultry below raw meats/seafood and all raw animal foods below ready-to-eat food. Keep chemicals separate from food.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Immediate Corrective Action

  • Reject deliveries that do not meet temperature, source, labeling, packaging, or quality standards.
  • If unsafe food was accepted by mistake, isolate it, label DO NOT USE, notify manager, and return or discard.
  • If shellfish tags are missing, reject the shellfish.
  • If storage order creates cross-contamination risk, move items immediately and sanitize affected shelves or containers.
  • If pest evidence is found in packaging or storage, isolate affected product and start pest corrective action.

Inspection Notes

  • Self-inspection items include shellfish not from approved source or missing tags, dirty/cracked eggs, potable water/ice source issues, food contamination, and pest evidence.
  • Inspectors may review receiving records, shellfish tags, storage order, labels, and evidence of approved source.
  • Unlabeled containers and food stored on the floor are common easy-to-catch findings.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Receiving log with date, supplier, product, temperature, accepted/rejected, and initials.
  • Rejected delivery notes with reason and manager approval.
  • Shellfish tags retained for 90 days.
  • Date labels and FIFO rotation records where used.
  • Corrective action records for storage or receiving failures.
