Harbor & Hearth Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-SANI-04

DOH-Informed · Cleaning & Sanitizing

DOH Warewashing & Sanitizing

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Cleaning & Sanitizing
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

NYC DOH source limits for manual warewashing, mechanical warewashing, wiping cloths, and food-contact surfaces.

Key Actions

  • Manual warewashing sequence is wash, rinse, sanitize, air dry.
  • Use the correct sanitizer strength for the method: immersion, wiping/spraying/pouring, wiping cloth storage, or dish machine.
  • High-temperature mechanical dish machines must meet the required final rinse and utensil surface temperature standards.
  • Never guess sanitizer strength by smell or color; use the correct test kit.
1

Purpose

Prevent contamination from dirty utensils, food-contact surfaces, wiping cloths, cutting boards, reusable containers, and failed warewashing equipment.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Cleaning & Sanitizing and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Manual warewashing requires a three-compartment sink unless a specific exception applies. Items must be washed, rinsed, sanitized, and air dried.
  • Hot-water manual sanitizing: immerse utensils in clean hot water at not less than 170°F for at least one-half minute.
  • Chlorine soaking/immersion: 50 ppm for 1 minute at 75°F to 140°F, using the concentration required by the Food Protection manual and product label.
  • Chlorine swabbing, wiping, spraying, or pouring: 100 ppm at 75°F to 140°F unless product label requires a different approved concentration.
  • Moist wiping cloths must be stored in sanitizer between uses; the Food Protection manual states this solution must be at least 50 ppm and changed frequently.
  • Iodine immersion target from the Food Protection manual: 12.5 ppm for 1 minute at 75°F or warmer, unless the product label requires otherwise.
  • Quaternary ammonium compounds must be used according to manufacturer label directions for concentration and contact time.
  • High-temperature mechanical dish machines must use a final rinse of at least 180°F and achieve a utensil surface temperature of at least 160°F.
  • Low-temperature chemical dish machines must deliver the sanitizer concentration required by the chemical label and must be checked with the correct test kit.
  • Only EPA-approved sanitizers and antimicrobial agents may be used on food and food-contact surfaces. Phenolic compounds may not be used for sanitizing food-contact surfaces.
  • Clean utensils must air dry. Towel drying can re-contaminate sanitized surfaces.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • At dish area setup before service.
  • Before food prep starts on any food-contact surface.
  • Between raw animal food and ready-to-eat food tasks.
  • After allergen contact, spills, pest evidence, or any contamination event.
  • Every time sanitizer buckets are changed or appear dirty, weak, too strong, or untestable.
  • At each dish machine check and at closing before clean equipment is stored.

Step 1 - Set up for the method used

For manual warewashing, prepare wash, rinse, and sanitize compartments. For machine warewashing, verify temperature gauges, sanitizer feed, test strips, and clean/dirty flow.

Step 2 - Wash before sanitizing

Remove all visible food, grease, and residue. Sanitizer does not work properly on dirty surfaces.

Step 3 - Rinse before sanitizing

Rinse detergent and loosened soil in clean water so sanitizer can contact the surface properly.

Step 4 - Sanitize with verified strength or temperature

Use hot water, chlorine, iodine, quaternary ammonium, or dish machine sanitizing at the required concentration, temperature, and contact time. Verify with test strips or a dishwasher thermometer as applicable.

Step 5 - Air dry and store protected

Do not towel dry. Store clean items inverted, covered, or otherwise protected from splash, handling, and dirty items.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Immediate Corrective Action

  • If sanitizer is below required range, remake solution and rewash/rinse/sanitize affected items.
  • If sanitizer is too strong, remake solution to the approved label range before use on food-contact surfaces.
  • If test strips are missing, stop chemical sanitizing until the correct test kit is available or use an approved hot-water method.
  • If a dish machine fails temperature or chemical checks, stop using it, switch to manual warewashing, label affected items for rewash, and call for repair.
  • If clean items are towel dried, splashed, or stored with dirty items, rewash, rinse, sanitize, and air dry.

Inspection Notes

  • Inspectors commonly check sanitizer buckets, test strips, three-compartment sink setup, dish machine gauges, final rinse temperature, air drying, and clean/dirty separation.
  • Self-inspection items include no facilities to wash/rinse/sanitize and food-contact surfaces not properly cleaned and sanitized.
  • Staff should know the sanitizer target range for the specific method they are using.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Sanitizer concentration log by method and station.
  • Dish machine temperature or chemical log.
  • Corrective action log for sanitizer failures, missing test kits, or dish machine downtime.
  • Pest/allergen/contamination cleaning records when applicable.
