Peaches Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-INSP-08

DOH-Informed · Manager Routine

DOH Inspection Readiness

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Manager Routine
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

Manager self-inspection routine organized around Article 81 critical areas and records inspectors commonly request.

Key Actions

  • Make sure a Food Protection Certificate holder is on premises and supervising food operations whenever required.
  • Walk temperatures, hand sinks, warewashing, pests, source control, and food protection first.
  • Keep FPC, pest contract, HACCP approvals, logs, shellfish tags, and corrective actions accessible.
  • Fix critical items immediately and document what changed.
1

Purpose

Prepare the restaurant to meet DOH expectations every day by turning inspection items into a repeatable manager routine.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Manager Routine and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

TermDefinition
SOPStandard Operating Procedure; the approved method for completing this task.
Person in ChargeThe manager or certified lead responsible for supervising the task and making food safety decisions during the shift.
Corrective ActionThe required response when a step, limit, record, or safety standard is missed or cannot be verified.
HACCPHazard Analysis and Critical Control Point; a written food safety plan required for certain specialized processes.
5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • A Food Protection Certificate holder must be on premises and supervise all food preparation activities during all hours of operation when required by Article 81.
  • Food Protection Certificate must be available for Department inspection.
  • Pest management contract and service records must be available.
  • Temperature logs, sanitizer logs, cooling logs, receiving records, shellfish tags, approved HACCP plans, and corrective actions must be current and accessible where used.
  • Critical food temperature, illness, handwashing, bare-hand contact, warewashing, pest, unsafe source, ROP/HACCP, and potable water issues require immediate action.
  • Suspected patron foodborne illness complaints and emergency occurrences must be escalated for required reporting and follow-up.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • Opening manager walk-through.
  • Pre-service line check.
  • Mid-service or shift-change spot check.
  • Closing verification.
  • Before expected inspections, after complaints, or after any major equipment/power/water failure.

Step 1 - Confirm certified supervision

Verify the Food Protection Certificate holder is present as required, knows current risk items, and can access records.

Step 2 - Start with critical food safety

Check hot/cold holding, cooling foods, reheating, cooking temps, thermometer availability, time-control labels, and ROP/HACCP restrictions.

Step 3 - Check people and handwashing

Verify hand sinks are within access, stocked, unobstructed, and used only for handwashing. Confirm staff are healthy and ready-to-eat food is protected from bare-hand contact.

Step 4 - Check cleaning, sanitizing, pests, and source

Verify sanitizer, warewashing, storage order, approved source, labels, food off the floor, chemical separation, pest evidence, garbage, drains, doors, and outside areas.

Step 5 - Review records

Confirm logs are complete, corrective actions are documented, shellfish tags are retained, pest records are available, and HACCP approvals are present for any ROP, cook-chill, or sous vide process.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Immediate Corrective Action

  • Correct critical violations immediately before normal service continues.
  • Assign a manager owner and deadline for non-critical repairs or documentation gaps.
  • Discard unsafe food, remove ill workers from food tasks, remake sanitizer, repair blocked hand sinks, stop unapproved ROP/HACCP processes, or call pest control as needed.
  • Document what was found, what was corrected, who approved it, and any follow-up date.
  • Use repeat findings as training topics at pre-shift or manager meetings.

Inspection Notes

  • The NYC self-inspection worksheet identifies weighted violation areas; starred/critical food safety items should be checked first.
  • Inspectors may ask for FPC coverage, logs, pest contract, HACCP approvals, shellfish tags, and proof of corrective action.
  • The goal is not paperwork alone: staff behavior and active conditions must match the records.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Food Protection Certificate coverage check.
  • Manager self-inspection checklist.
  • Temperature, cooling, reheating, receiving, sanitizer, and corrective action logs.
  • Pest management contract and service records.
  • Approved HACCP plans for ROP, cook-chill, or sous vide if used.
  • Shellfish tags retained for 90 days.
  • Employee illness, patron illness complaint, and emergency occurrence notes.
  • Repair and maintenance tickets.
