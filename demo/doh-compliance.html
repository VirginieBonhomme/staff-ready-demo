Publicly available NYC DOH food safety materials translated into operating SOPs for daily control, training, inspection prep, and manager follow-up. This is an operating guide, not legal or regulatory advice.

Key Actions

  • Use the listed NYC DOH source limits for cooking temperatures, time control, ROP/HACCP, sanitizing, employee illness, and consumer advisory.
  • Train staff on the SOPs that match their role and shift responsibilities.
  • Keep required logs current so corrective action can be proven, not just described.
  • Escalate critical failures before service continues: temperature abuse, illness, hand sink failure, sanitizer failure, pests, unsafe source, or unapproved ROP/HACCP.
1

What This Library Covers

This library is built from the NYC Food Protection Course Manual, the NYC Food Protection study guide, NYC Health Code Article 81 references, Chapter 23 references, and the NYC Food Establishment Self-Inspection Worksheet.

2

Critical Limits Snapshot

  • Cold food that requires time/temperature control for safety: 41°F or below; processed/smoked fish: 38°F or below; intact raw shell eggs: 45°F or below.
  • Hot holding: 140°F or above.
  • Cooking temperatures vary by food: poultry 165°F, injected/tenderized meat 155°F, ground meat 158°F, shell eggs 145°F, pork Article 81 minimum 150°F with 155°F house target from the self-inspection worksheet, and roasts by the Article 81 time-temperature table.
  • Reheating for hot holding: 165°F for 15 seconds within 2 hours.
  • Cooling: 140°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then 70°F to 41°F within 4 additional hours.
  • Time control: cold foods need 4-hour check and 6-hour discard; hot foods need 4-hour discard.
  • ROP, cook-chill, and sous vide require prior Department-approved HACCP plan.
  • Manual warewashing: wash, rinse, sanitize, air dry. Hot-water sanitizing is 170°F for at least 30 seconds.
  • Chlorine immersion is 50 ppm for 1 minute; chlorine wiping/spraying/pouring is 100 ppm; wiping cloth storage must be at least 50 ppm.
  • Food Protection Certificate holder must be on premises and supervising food preparation activities during all hours of operation when required.
  • Written consumer advisory is required for raw, undercooked, or cooked-to-order animal foods.

Records To Keep

  • Daily temperature logs
  • Cooking, cooling, reheating, and time-control records where applicable
  • Receiving logs and rejected delivery notes
  • Shellfish tags retained for 90 days
  • Sanitizer concentration and dish machine logs
  • Corrective action records
  • Pest control contract, service records, and follow-up notes
  • FPC coverage and inspection readiness checks
  • Approved HACCP plans for ROP, cook-chill, or sous vide if used
  • Current menu copy with consumer advisory language
