Mid-Service Reference

Crisis

Use this page when something is urgent. Call 911 first for immediate danger, then tell your manager. Find the closest situation below and follow the steps in order.

911
Immediate danger? Call 911 now. Do not wait for permission. Do not attempt to manage a life-safety emergency yourself.
Call 911
Fire Burn Knife wound Fall Allergy Customer unwell Fridge issue Dish machine

Physical Safety

Fire

Oil or fryer fire

  1. Turn off the heat source immediately — do not move the pan.
  2. Never use water on an oil fire. Use the fire blanket to smother it.
  3. If it does not go out within seconds, activate the fire alarm and evacuate.
  4. Call 911. Do not re-enter the building.
  5. Tell your manager once you are safely outside.

Always call manager. Even if you put it out — a fire that was suppressed must be reported and the equipment inspected before it is used again.

Injury

Burn

  1. Cool the burn under cold running water for a minimum of 20 minutes. Start immediately.
  2. Do not apply ice, butter, oil, or any cream.
  3. Do not pop blisters or remove clothing stuck to the burn.
  4. Cover loosely with a clean non-fluffy material — not cotton wool.
  5. Tell your manager. For anything larger than a palm, call 911.

Always call manager. All burns are recorded in the accident log before end of shift.

Injury

Laceration or knife wound

  1. Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth. Hold it — do not keep checking.
  2. Elevate the hand or arm above heart level if possible.
  3. If bleeding is severe, does not slow within 5 minutes, or the wound is deep — call 911.
  4. Tell your manager immediately.
  5. Do not return to food handling until the wound is fully covered with a blue detectable plaster.

Always call manager. Blue detectable plasters are in the first aid kit. If the plaster comes off during service, stop and replace it immediately.

Injury

Slip, trip, or fall

  1. Do not move the person if they hit their head or complain of neck or back pain. Call 911.
  2. If they are conscious and there is no head or spinal concern, help them to a safe position.
  3. Tell your manager immediately.
  4. Identify and address the hazard — wet floor, spilled oil — before anyone else walks through it.

Always call manager. All falls are recorded in the accident log. The hazard that caused it is your manager's responsibility to address — flag it clearly.

Food Safety

Allergen

Customer flags an allergy after ordering

  1. Stop the ticket immediately. Do not let the dish reach the pass.
  2. Tell your manager or head chef now — before anything else.
  3. Do not guess whether the dish is safe. The answer comes from someone who knows the recipes and prep.
  4. If the dish has already been served, tell your manager before the customer eats further.
  5. Do not attempt to remove an allergen from a dish that already contains it. Discard and remake.

Always call manager. An incorrect "it should be fine" is the most dangerous thing you can say in this situation. Your uncertainty is not the problem — acting on it without checking is.

Food Safety

Customer reports feeling unwell

  1. Tell your manager immediately. Do not attempt to manage this yourself.
  2. Note the table number, what was ordered, and what time they ate.
  3. Do not discard the food. Set aside any remaining portion for potential investigation.
  4. Do not speak to the customer about the cause — that is the manager's conversation.
  5. If the customer requests emergency services, call 911.

Always call manager. If more than one table reports illness from the same service, the manager must consider contacting Environmental Health. That decision is theirs, not yours.

Food Safety

Fridge or freezer out of safe range

  1. Note the exact temperature and the current time. Write it down.
  2. Tell your manager immediately.
  3. Do not move or serve any food from the unit until the manager decides.
  4. Do not turn the unit off and back on hoping it resets — leave it running and let the manager assess.
  5. If the unit has been out of range for an unknown period, assume product loss. Do not serve.

Always call manager. Safe range: fridge ≤ 41°F, freezer ≤ 0°F. Food held above 46°F for more than 4 hours total must be discarded — that decision is your manager's to make with the full facts.

Food Safety

Cross-contamination suspected

  1. Stop service of any affected dishes immediately.
  2. Tell your manager now — before any further items go out.
  3. Do not attempt to "fix" or re-plate a contaminated dish. Discard it.
  4. Remove any potentially contaminated prep, boards, or utensils from service.
  5. Sanitize the affected station before it is used again.

Always call manager. Cross-contamination is especially critical for allergens — if raw allergen has contacted food for an allergic customer, that customer must be told and the dish remade from scratch on a clean station.

Operational

Equipment

Equipment failure mid-service

  1. Stop using the equipment. Turn it off at the isolator if possible.
  2. Tell your manager immediately.
  3. Do not attempt to repair, reset, or diagnose it yourself.
  4. Communicate to the pass what you can and cannot produce right now.
  5. Menu adaptation is your manager's call — wait for direction before changing any dishes.

Always call manager. Equipment that trips a breaker or produces unusual sounds, smells, or sparks must not be restarted without a manager inspection first.

Equipment

Dish machine failure

  1. Switch to three-sink sanitizing immediately. Do not wait to be told.
  2. Tell your manager — they need to know about the impact on plate and equipment supply.
  3. Do not serve food on plates that have been washed in cold water only.
  4. Prioritise the items service needs most — communicate what is ready and when.

Always call manager. Three-sink sanitizing procedure is at sops/three-sink-sanitizing. Know it before you need it.

Building

Power outage

  1. Do not open fridge or freezer doors. Temperature holds for up to 4 hours if sealed.
  2. Turn off all cooking equipment at the isolator — do not leave them live for when power returns.
  3. Tell your manager immediately.
  4. Do not serve cold food that has been at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
  5. Wait for manager instruction before restarting equipment when power returns — restart in sequence, not all at once.

Always call manager. After power is restored, check all fridge and freezer temperatures before using any stored food. Log the readings.

Service

Out of a key ingredient mid-service

  1. Tell your manager or head chef immediately — do not wait until it becomes a problem on a ticket.
  2. Check the walk-in and dry store before confirming it is gone.
  3. Do not substitute an ingredient without the head chef's sign-off — especially anything allergen-relevant.
  4. The decision to 86 a dish is the manager's. Give them the information and wait for direction.

Always call manager. The worst outcome is a ticket that cannot be fulfilled because the information was not passed on early enough. Early communication gives the kitchen options. Late communication does not.
