Staff Ready Ops. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-TEMP-01

DOH-Informed · Food Temperature

DOH Time & Temperature Control

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Food Temperature
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

NYC DOH source limits for food temperature for items that require time/temperature control for safety, cooking, holding, reheating, cooling, thawing, and time as a public health control.

Key Actions

  • Use the listed cooking temperature for the food type; do not rely on one generic cooking number.
  • Keep cold food that requires time/temperature control for safety at 41°F or below and hot food that requires time/temperature control for safety at 140°F or above.
  • Use time as a public health control only with the required start temperature, marking, checks, and discard limits.
  • Do not conduct ROP, cook-chill, or sous vide without prior Department approval and an approved HACCP plan.
1

Purpose

Prevent pathogen growth and survival by controlling food temperature for items that require time/temperature control for safety through receiving, preparation, cooking, cooling, reheating, hot holding, cold holding, thawing, and time-controlled service.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Food Temperature and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • Clean and calibrated probe thermometer.
  • Temperature log, cooking log, time-control labels, and corrective action log.
  • Working refrigeration, hot holding, cooking, cooling, and reheating equipment as applicable.
  • Approved HACCP records for reduced oxygen packaging, cook-chill, or sous vide if used.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • At receiving for refrigerated, frozen, and hot delivered foods.
  • At opening before food is loaded into holding units.
  • Before serving any food cooked to order that has a required minimum temperature.
  • Before placing cooked food into hot holding.
  • During every meal period for cold holding, hot holding, and prep areas.
  • At the 4-hour mark for cold food held by time as a public health control.
  • Whenever equipment alarms, power is interrupted, doors are left open, or food has unknown time/temperature history.

Step 1 - Identify the food category

Before cooking or reheating, identify whether the item is poultry, stuffed food, ground meat, pork, shell egg, roast, microwave-cooked raw animal food, commercial precooked food, or another item that requires time/temperature control for safety.

Step 2 - Verify with a clean probe thermometer

Probe the thickest or coldest part of the food. Do not rely only on color, cook time, steam, or an equipment display.

Step 3 - Control holding equipment

Preheat hot holding units and verify refrigeration is working before loading food. Keep backup product under temperature control until needed.

Step 4 - Use time control only when marked

Mark the start/removal time, required check time if cold-held, and discard time. Unmarked time-control food must be discarded if timing cannot be proven.

Step 5 - Escalate ROP requests

Do not vacuum package, cook-chill, sous vide, or otherwise use ROP processes unless the manager confirms Department-approved HACCP documentation is in place.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Critical Limits From NYC DOH Materials

  • Temperature danger zone: 41°F to 140°F. Food that requires time/temperature control for safety must be kept outside this range except during necessary preparation, cooling, reheating, or approved time control.
  • Cold holding: 41°F or below. Processed or smoked fish must be held at 38°F or below. Intact raw shell eggs may be held at 45°F or below.
  • Hot holding: 140°F or above. Hot holding equipment must be preheated before food is placed inside.
  • Poultry, poultry parts, ground poultry, food containing poultry, poultry stuffing, and stuffed poultry: 165°F for 15 seconds.
  • Stuffed meats, stuffed fish, stuffed ratites, and stuffing containing ratites or fish: 165°F. Mechanically tenderized or injected meats: 155°F.
  • Ground and comminuted meat other than poultry: 158°F, unless otherwise ordered by the consumer and covered by the required consumer advisory.
  • Pork and food containing pork, other than whole pork roasts and ground/comminuted pork: Article 81 lists 150°F for 15 seconds unless otherwise ordered by the consumer. The NYC self-inspection worksheet uses 155°F as the inspection threshold, so use 155°F as the house target unless the manager/PIC approves a documented exception.
  • Unpasteurized raw shell eggs and foods containing unpasteurized shell eggs: 145°F for 15 seconds, unless prepared below that temperature at the consumer request and covered by the required consumer advisory.
  • Raw animal foods cooked in a microwave: cover, rotate or stir, heat to at least 165°F, and allow a covered 2-minute standing time.
  • Reheating for hot holding: previously cooked and cooled food that requires time/temperature control for safety must reach 165°F for 15 seconds within 2 hours and then be held at 140°F or above.
  • Commercially processed precooked food that requires time/temperature control for safety in intact packages or hermetically sealed containers must be heated to 140°F within 2 hours if hot held.
  • Thaw food that requires time/temperature control for safety under refrigeration at 41°F or below, under running potable water at 70°F or below, in a microwave as part of immediate cooking, or as part of the cooking process.
  • Time as a public health control: cold food must start at 41°F or below and hot food must start at 140°F or above; food cannot be returned to temperature control to extend use.
  • Cold ready-to-eat food that requires time/temperature control for safety under time control: mark removal/start time, 4-hour check time, and 6-hour discard time; discard if temperature is over 70°F at 4 hours or if not served within 6 hours.
  • Hot food that requires time/temperature control for safety under time control: mark removal/start time and 4-hour discard time; cook, serve, or discard within 4 hours.
  • Reduced oxygen packaging, cook-chill, and sous vide require prior Department approval of a HACCP plan for each food item or category.

Immediate Corrective Action

  • If a cooking temperature is below the listed limit, continue cooking and recheck with a clean thermometer before service.
  • If cold food is above 41°F, smoked fish is above 38°F, hot food is below 140°F, or time/temperature history is unknown, isolate the food and notify the manager/PIC.
  • Discard time-controlled cold food if it is over 70°F at 4 hours or not served within 6 hours; discard time-controlled hot food if not served within 4 hours.
  • If food was returned to temperature control to extend time-control use, discard it.
  • If ROP, cook-chill, or sous vide is being done without approved HACCP documentation, stop the process, isolate affected food, and escalate to ownership/management.
  • Document item, temperature, time found, action taken, manager initials, and discarded amount.

Inspection Notes

  • Self-inspection worksheet items include cooking, hot holding, reheating, cold holding, ROP, and thermometer violations.
  • Inspectors may ask staff to explain the required cooking temperature for a specific item.
  • Menu items served raw or undercooked require the consumer advisory covered in the DOH Consumer Advisory SOP.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Daily temperature log for each unit and holding station.
  • Cooking temperature log by menu item category.
  • Time-control labels or batch sheets with start, check, and discard times.
  • Corrective action log for out-of-range food or equipment.
  • Approved HACCP documentation for ROP, cook-chill, or sous vide if used.
