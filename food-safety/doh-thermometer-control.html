Staff Ready Ops. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
DOH-THERM-03

DOH-Informed · Measuring Temperatures

DOH Thermometer Control

Department / Area DOH-Informed · Measuring Temperatures
Prepared By Manager / PIC
Date Reviewed June 2026
Version 1.0

Thermometer availability, proper probe use, cleaning, calibration, and verification routines.

Key Actions

  • Keep an accurate probe thermometer available wherever food that requires time/temperature control for safety is handled.
  • Clean and sanitize the probe before and after each use.
  • Probe the thickest or coldest part of the food and wait for the reading to stabilize.
  • Calibrate when dropped, after extreme temperature changes, or when accuracy is questionable.
1

Purpose

Make sure food safety decisions are based on accurate product temperatures rather than guesses, surface readings, or equipment displays.

2

Scope

Applies to DOH-Informed · Measuring Temperatures and any staff member assigned to this process, task, equipment, checklist, or manager follow-up.

3

Responsibilities

  • All assigned staff must follow this SOP exactly as written during the applicable task or shift.
  • The responsible manager or team (Manager / PIC) is responsible for training staff, answering questions, and verifying that the procedure is followed.
  • The shift lead or manager must stop the task and start corrective action when the standard cannot be met.
4

Definitions / Key Terms

5

Materials / Equipment Needed

  • An appropriately scaled metal-stem thermometer, thermocouple, or digital thermometer must be available and used for cooking, cooling, reheating, and holding checks.
  • Glass thermometers are not allowed in food service operations.
  • Bi-metallic stem thermometers must be inserted to the dimple or sensing area; digital/thermistor probes usually need about 3/4 inch inserted unless manufacturer instructions say otherwise.
  • Equipment thermometers must be present in refrigerators, freezers, and hot holding units and placed where they reflect the warmest or most representative area.
  • Ice-point calibration target is 32°F. Boiling-point calibration target is 212°F at sea level, adjusted for altitude if needed.
6

Procedure (Step-by-Step)

When To Check

  • At opening, before the first temperature log is completed.
  • Before cooking, cooling, reheating, or holding decisions are accepted.
  • After a thermometer is dropped, damaged, exposed to extreme heat/cold, or gives a suspicious reading.
  • Whenever a manager reviews a temperature-related corrective action.

Step 1 - Inspect thermometer condition

Confirm the probe is clean, intact, readable, and appropriate for the food being measured.

Step 2 - Sanitize before use

Use an alcohol wipe or approved sanitizer. Do not insert a contaminated probe into ready-to-eat food.

Step 3 - Probe correctly

Insert into the thickest part, center, or coldest spot. Avoid touching pan sides, bones, or heating surfaces. Wait until the reading stabilizes.

Step 4 - Calibrate as needed

Use an ice-water slurry for 32°F calibration or another approved method. Adjust or remove from service if it cannot be corrected.

Step 5 - Store safely

Clean, sanitize, dry, and store the thermometer in a protected location, not loose in a drawer with dirty utensils.

7

Safety & Compliance Considerations

Immediate Corrective Action

  • If no working food thermometer is available, stop temperature-dependent cooking, cooling, reheating, and holding decisions until one is available.
  • If a thermometer is inaccurate and cannot be calibrated, remove it from service and replace it.
  • If food temperatures were logged with a faulty thermometer, recheck affected food and document the correction.
  • If equipment thermometer is missing or inaccurate, place a working thermometer in the unit and replace the equipment thermometer.

Inspection Notes

  • Self-inspection item 4J covers missing or unused food thermometers for cooking, cooling, reheating, and holding.
  • Item 10E covers missing accurate thermometers in refrigerated or hot holding equipment.
  • Inspectors may ask a staff member to demonstrate thermometer cleaning and use.
8

Quality Control / Verification

  • Confirm each step was completed in the required order and within the required standard.
  • Manager or shift lead verifies the finished work before the station, item, or record is released for use.
  • Correct and document any missed step, unsafe condition, incomplete record, or unclear responsibility.
9

Documentation & Records

  • Thermometer calibration log.
  • Daily temperature logs showing initials and product temperatures.
  • Corrective action records for faulty or missing thermometers.
  • Equipment service notes for units with missing or failed thermometers.
