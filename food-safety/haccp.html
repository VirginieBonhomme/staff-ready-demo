HACCP Plan

Manager / Head Chef Updated: May 2026 Category: Food Safety

Hazard Analysis & Critical Control Points

HACCP is the food safety management system required by law for all commercial food businesses. It identifies where food safety hazards can occur in our processes, establishes controls to prevent them, and defines what to do when those controls fail. This page is the working summary of our HACCP plan. All staff must understand the Critical Control Points (CCPs) that apply to their role.

The 7 Principles
1. Hazard Analysis — Identify all biological, chemical, and physical hazards at each stage of food handling
2. Identify CCPs — Determine which steps are Critical Control Points where a hazard can be prevented or eliminated
3. Set Critical Limits — Define the min/max value (e.g., temperature) that must be met at each CCP
4. Monitoring — Establish how each CCP will be monitored, how often, and by whom
5. Corrective Actions — Define what to do when monitoring shows a CCP is out of control
6. Verification — Confirm the HACCP system is working — through audits, reviews, and testing
7. Record Keeping — Document all monitoring results, corrective actions, and verification activities
Our Critical Control Points

CCP 1 — Receiving

Hazard: Pathogen growth, chemical contamination, or physical contamination in incoming goods
Critical limit: Chilled goods ≤ 46°F on arrival. Frozen goods solidly frozen. No damaged packaging. Valid use-by dates present.
Monitoring: Probe temperature of each chilled category on every delivery. Visual inspection of all packaging.
Corrective action: Reject non-compliant goods. Log rejection on Receiving Log.
Record: Receiving Log — every delivery

CCP 2 — Cold Storage

Hazard: Pathogen growth due to temperature abuse in fridges and freezers
Critical limit: Cold food ≤ 41°F. Frozen food ≤ 0°F. Hot food ≥ 140°F.
Monitoring: Temperature check at opening, during service, and at closing. Probe check of a mid-shelf food item at opening.
Corrective action: Food above 46°F — do not use. Alert manager. Arrange equipment repair. See Corrective Action SOP.
Record: Daily Temperature Log

CCP 3 — Cooking

Hazard: Survival of pathogens due to insufficient cooking
Critical limit: Poultry ≥ 165°F core. Fish ≥ 145°F core. Whole cuts of beef/lamb ≥ 145°F core. Minced meat ≥ 165°F core.
Monitoring: Probe every portion of high-risk proteins at the time of cooking. Calibrated probe thermometer only.
Corrective action: Return to heat until minimum temperature is reached. Do not serve. Log the corrective action.
Record: Cook temperature noted on Daily Temperature Log where applicable

CCP 4 — Cooling

Hazard: Rapid pathogen growth during slow cooling of cooked food
Critical limit: Cool from above 140°F to below 70°F within 2 hours, then to 41°F or below within 4 hours total.
Monitoring: Probe food during cooling at 30-minute intervals. Record start temperature and times.
Corrective action: Food not at 41°F within 4 hours — discard. Log the corrective action.
Record: Daily Temperature Log — cooling records section

CCP 5 — Reheating

Hazard: Pathogen survival in previously cooked food not reheated to a safe temperature
Critical limit: All reheated food must reach ≥ 165°F throughout. Must be reheated only once.
Monitoring: Probe the thickest part of every reheated batch before placing in service or hot hold.
Corrective action: Return to heat until 165°F is reached. If fails a second time, discard. Log the corrective action.
Record: Reheat temperature noted on Daily Temperature Log

CCP 6 — Allergen Control

Hazard: Allergenic protein transferred to a guest who has declared an allergy — potentially fatal
Critical limit: Zero cross-contact. Allergen-modified dishes must be prepared with dedicated clean equipment and confirmed allergen-free from source to plate.
Monitoring: Allergen flag on every ticket triggers the Allergen Handling SOP. Verbal confirmation on the pass before service.
Corrective action: Any doubt about a dish — pull it and remake from scratch. Never serve a dish you are not certain is allergen-safe.
Record: Allergen incidents logged in the incident log
Record Keeping Requirements
Daily Temperature Log — completed every day, retained for 3 months minimum
Receiving Log — completed for every delivery, retained for 3 months minimum
Sanitizer Log — completed at each solution test, retained for 3 months minimum
Corrective Action Log — completed same day as any failure, retained for 3 months minimum
Pest Control Reports — filed after each contractor visit, retained for 12 months minimum
Thermometer Calibration Log — completed daily, retained for 3 months minimum
